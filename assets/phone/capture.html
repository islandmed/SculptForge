Scan an object

Walk slowly all the way round, then once more a little lower.

  • 20-60 seconds, slow and steady (no quick turns)
  • Even, soft light; no harsh shadows or reflections
  • Keep the object in the middle of the screen and the whole of it in view
  • Don't zoom; keep about the same distance all the way
  • A plain table or floor around it helps

One photo to 3D

  • One object, the whole of it in the picture
  • A plain background (a wall, a sheet of paper)
  • Soft light; a three-quarter view shows the most

Your computer sends it to the image-to-3D service chosen in SculptForge (Generate > Image to 3D; free Hugging Face by default).

Send a splat file

Already scanned it with a splat app? Export a Gaussian splat .ply and send it here:

  • Scaniverse: open the scan, Share, Export Model, PLY (splat)
  • Polycam: open the capture, Export, Gaussian splat, PLY
  • Luma: open the capture, download, Gaussian splat (.ply)

Save it to Files first if the app can't share straight to the browser. .splat and .spz work too.

Face capture

Your phone's front camera tracks 52 facial expressions (the ARKit set) and sends them to SculptForge about 30 times a second.

  • The video never leaves your phone: only the expression numbers do
  • Prop the phone up at eye level, about an arm's length away
  • Light on your face, not behind you

The camera needs a secure page, so this opens SculptForge's face page on its website, which then talks straight to your computer over Wi-Fi.

Can't reach your computer

SculptForge isn't answering.
  • Is the Capture from phone window still open in SculptForge?
  • Phone and computer on the same Wi-Fi (not a guest network, not mobile data)
  • A VPN on either device can block local connections
  • On Windows, allow SculptForge on private networks in the firewall
