Parts

A scene in SculptForge can hold several separate meshes, called Parts (experienced sculptors: Parts): a body, eyes, teeth, a helmet and a sword can each live in their own Part. This chapter covers the Parts list, selecting, adding, hiding, reordering, merging and splitting Parts, and how they travel through files.

How Parts work#

  • Only the active Part is sculpted, painted, masked or changed by a command. The others are drawn slightly darker and greyer so you can see which one you are working on.
  • Each Part keeps its own mesh, mask, vertex paint, face groups, hidden faces, UVs and sculpt layers.
  • Each Part has its own undo history. Ctrl+Z undoes the last change in the active Part, and switching Parts doesn't lose the history of the others.
  • Adding, deleting, reordering, merging and splitting Parts are not undoable, except Split hidden, Split masked and Split unmasked (one undo step each). Merges and deletes ask for confirmation first.
  • The Parts list highlights the active Part. In the Classic layout the title strip also shows it and its place in the list, for example Part Helmet (2/5).

The Parts list#

The list is in Tool > Parts, which is open in the right tray by default.

Each row shows a small picture of the Part, its name, its triangle count, its shading and an eye. The pictures are drawn from the model the viewport already has on the graphics card, so they cost almost nothing even on very dense Parts, and they catch up a moment after you stop changing a Part (not during a stroke).

ToDo this
Select a PartClick its row, or Alt+click it in the viewport; triple-click another Part in the viewport to select it and frame it
Sculpt the Part under the cursorPoint at it and press Alt+Q (every input style, as in Blender): it becomes the active Part, the view stays put and each Part keeps its mask
Rename itDouble-click the row (or right-click > Rename), type, press Enter
Hide or show itClick the eye at the end of the row
Shade it smooth or flatClick the mark left of the eye: it goes from as the scene (dim) to Smooth (a disc) to Flat (a faceted hexagon) and back. Only this Part changes; the scene's own setting is Render > Materials > Shading
Duplicate or delete itRight-click the row > Duplicate (Ctrl+Shift+D) or Delete...
Make a copy that shares the meshRight-click the row > Linked Duplicate, or the Linked Duplicate button; see Linked Duplicates
See its vertex and triangle countsPoint at the row for a moment

The list shows six rows and scrolls when there are more. Names are kept unique: renaming a Part to a name that is already taken adds a number.

Selecting a hidden Part shows it again, because you can't sculpt what you can't see.

Select a Part in the viewport#

Hold Alt and click another Part on the canvas. It becomes the active one and the list scrolls to it.

Alt+click only selects when you click a Part other than the active one and release without moving. If you drag instead, Alt keeps its usual meaning: an inverted stroke on the active Part, or a pan when you start off the model.

Add a Part#

Click Append and choose:

  • Sphere, Cube or Plane: a new primitive placed beside the visible model (to the right, on +X), sized to about a third of the largest visible Part.
  • Insert on the model: choose a shape, then drag on the active Part's surface to add it there as a new face group (the drag sets its size and turns it). With Symmetry X, Y or Z on, a mirrored copy goes on the other side too, in the same undo step; a shape inserted right on a mirror plane isn't doubled. To insert your own mesh, choose Active Part's copy... and pick another Part (it stays as it is), or From file... and pick an .obj, .ply or .glb file; then drag as with the shapes. The mesh is centred, scaled so its largest side matches the drag, and stood on the surface along its own up (+Y) axis. Esc, any brush, or Draw / Move stops inserting. (Command: insert_mesh part=<name> or insert_mesh file=<path>.) To insert from a kitbash set, choose Insert set from file... (one .obj or .glb with several objects) or Insert set from folder... (its .obj, .ply and .glb files, by name; not its subfolders). The Insert set window shows a thumbnail of each item: click one, then drag on the model as above. While inserting, ] steps to the next item and [ to the previous one (around the set; brush size keys come back once you stop). Esc or the window's close button stops. (Commands: insert_set_items set=<path> lists them; insert_mesh set=<path> index=<n> inserts item n, 0 being the first.) Creature bones, at the end of the same menu, is a set that comes with SculptForge: no file to pick (see below).
  • From file...: a model (.obj, .fbx, .glb, .gltf, .ply, .stl, Bridge files, .usda, .usd, .usdz) or a project (.sfproj). A file with several objects adds one Part per object.

The new Part becomes the active one.

Creature bones#

Append > Insert on the model > Creature bones opens a built-in insert set of original fantasy creature bones to start from: Skull, Jaw, Fang, Blade tooth, Swept horn, Spiral horn, Claw, Vertebra, Tail vertebrae, Rib, Limb bone, Wing bone and Wing finger. Click one in the Insert set window and drag on the model, as with any set; horns, claws and teeth grow from where you drag. With Symmetry X on, a horn or a claw gets its mirrored twin.

Each piece is a clean, closed quad mesh of 500 to 4,200 faces, so it takes Add Level and sculpting well: insert, then sculpt the joins smooth, or Voxel Remesh to fuse the piece into the model. Drag pieces onto an empty Plane or Sphere Part to build a skeleton of their own Part. The pieces were designed for SculptForge (tools/creature-bones in the source) and come under the app's licence, GPL-3.0. (Commands: insert_set_items set=builtin:creature-bones; insert_mesh set=builtin:creature-bones index=<n>.)

You can also add files as Parts from elsewhere:

  • Import in the Tool palette header (or File > Import) adds a model file as a new Part.
  • In the Library Tool tab, Shift+double-click a model, or right-click it and choose Append as Part.

Tip: Duplicate (Ctrl+Shift+D, also in the command palette as Duplicate Part) copies the active Part with its layers and attributes, names it <name> copy and puts it right under the original. Use it to keep a safe copy before a destructive step such as a remesh. Linked Duplicate instead makes a copy that shares the mesh and changes with it (rivets, legs, columns); see Linked Duplicates.

Show, hide and solo#

  • The eye on each row shows or hides that Part.
  • Solo shows only the active Part. Click it again to bring the others back. The Solo button on the right shelf does the same.
  • X-Ray, next to Solo, shows the active Part through the others: the other Parts turn see-through and only their nearest surface shows. When only the active Part is shown, it is see-through itself. Click X-Ray again to make them solid. View > X-Ray does the same.
  • Unhide all shows every Part.

Hidden Parts aren't drawn, can't be selected with Alt+click, and are left out of exports.

Note: Part visibility is different from hiding part of a mesh with face groups or a rectangle. That hides faces inside one Part; see Face Groups and visibility.

Folders#

Folders group Parts in the list, for example the eyes and teeth of a head, so you can hide, show and move them together.

ToDo this
Make a folderClick the folder button under the list: a new folder holds the active Part; type its name and press Enter
Put a Part in a folderDrag its row onto the folder's header (or onto a Part already in it), or right-click the row > Folder > the folder's name
Take a Part outDrag it onto a Part outside folders, or right-click it > Folder > Out of the folder
Fold or open a folderClick its header
Rename itDouble-click the header (or right-click > Rename)
Hide or show all its PartsClick the eye on the header
Move, turn or scale itRight-click the header > Move with the gizmo
Remove itRight-click the header > Remove folder (keep its Parts)

A Part that joins a folder moves to just below the folder's last Part, so a folder's Parts stay together. When the folder holding the active Part is folded, its header shows the accent bar. Move with the gizmo makes one of the folder's Parts active and turns on the gizmo's Folder option, so every visible Part in the folder moves together; see All Parts and Folder. Duplicates and the pieces of a split stay in their Part's folder. Folders are saved in the project; older versions of SculptForge open the file and simply show no folders.

Reorder Parts#

The up and down arrow buttons under the list move the active Part one place up or down. The order matters for Merge down and is the order objects are written to files.

Merge Parts#

  • Merge down... merges the active Part with the one below it.
  • Merge visible... merges every visible Part into one new Part called Merged. Hidden Parts stay as they are.

Merging keeps every attribute, gives each original mesh its own face groups (so you can split them again later) and matches sculpt layers by name. The merged Part starts with an empty undo history. A Part with subdivision levels is merged (or split) at the level it is on, which becomes the base of the result; the status line names the Parts that lost their levels.

Painted textures are kept too: the merged Part gets new Auto UVs and one texture, the size of the largest, holding each part's paint exactly (texture layers are flattened; parts without a texture are white). On big meshes the Auto UV takes a while. Scripts can skip it with keep_texture=false, which drops the textures.

Tip: Merge first when you want one voxel remesh to fuse several pieces into a single watertight surface.

Pose all Parts#

A character made of many Parts (body, eyes, teeth, armour plates, belts) can be posed in one go instead of Part by Part:

  1. Click Pose all Parts under the Parts list. The visible Parts are merged into one light grey Pose Mesh (300,000 triangles in all, shared by size, so small Parts such as eyes keep their shape) and hidden while you pose. Each Part is one face group on it, so Mask by Face group picks whole Parts. A Part with subdivision levels joins from a lower level, so even a heavy figure is ready in seconds.
  2. Pose it with the Gizmo (W move, E scale, R rotate) and masks: mask the body, rotate an arm about the shoulder with a falloff for a soft joint. Symmetry poses both sides. The Pose Mesh is small, so every drag is instant, and Ctrl+Z steps back through the pose.
  3. Click Apply Pose. Every Part takes the same moves at full resolution, its sculpt layers, subdivision levels and micro level following; the Pose Mesh goes away. The whole pose is one undo step across every Part. Cancel Pose removes the Pose Mesh and changes nothing.

Only Gizmo moves carry over for now: a brush stroke on the Pose Mesh stops Apply until you undo it. A Part you edit while posing (by selecting it in the list) keeps your edit and is left out of the pose; the status line says which. A linked copy is left out too (shown, not posed): posing it would pose every copy; Make Unique first. Scripts: figure_pose_begin triangles=300000, figure_pose_apply, figure_pose_cancel, figure_pose_info.

Split a Part#

  • Split groups makes one Part per face group. It is available when the active Part has more than one face group.
  • Split parts makes one Part per disconnected piece, largest first.

Both replace the active Part with the pieces. Because a merge keeps each original mesh in its own face group, Split groups undoes a merge.

Split hidden, masked or unmasked#

The Split row (Hidden, Masked, Unmasked) takes part of the active Part out into a new Part just below it, in the same folder, shown as the source is and shaded the same way. Each is one undo step: Ctrl+Z puts the triangles back and removes the new Part.

ButtonMoves into the new PartCommand
Split > Hiddenthe hidden triangles; they are shown in the new Partpart_split_hidden
Split > Maskedthe masked triangles; the new Part starts unmaskedpart_split_masked
Split > Unmaskedthe triangles that are not masked; the masked ones staypart_split_unmasked
Keep original (checkbox below)a copy of those triangles; the source Part stays as it is, levels and all, and undo just removes the copykeep=true on any of the three

A triangle counts as masked when the average mask of its three corners is above 50%, so a soft mask edge splits along its middle and a single masked corner doesn't take its neighbours. UVs, face groups, vertex paint and sculpt layers carry over to both sides. Subdivision levels can't be cut: the split is made at the level the Part is on, which becomes its base (undo brings the levels back). To delete instead of split, use Remove Hidden or Delete masked in Tool > Visibility.

Extract a shell#

Make Shell, in Tool > Parts > Extract, makes a new Part from the masked part of the active one: a helmet from the top of a head, a strap, a sleeve, a piece of armour that fits the surface exactly. It works:

  1. Mask the part (hold Ctrl and paint, or use Rectangle Mask / Lasso Mask; see Masking).
  2. Click Make Shell. The shell appears as a preview, a Part just below the active one.
  3. Change Thickness, Edge Smooth, Both Sides, Corners or Border and the preview follows. Click Accept to keep it.
SettingWhat it does
ThicknessThe shell's thickness as a share of the Part's size: 0.015 (the default) is 1.5%. Negative grows it inward; 0 makes a single-sided surface
Edge SmoothSmooths the extracted edge, which otherwise follows the mask's triangles in steps. 0 keeps it as masked
Both SidesGrows the shell both inward and outward from the surface
CornersTriangles instead of quads in the rim where the edge turns sharply (over 50 degrees), so tight corners don't get twisted quads
BorderThe whole rim is triangles instead of quads

The new Part has three face groups, the outer surface, the inner one and the rim between them (a band of quads), so you can mask, hide or split them apart. Vertex Paint comes with it; hidden faces are left out. Accept is one undo step: Ctrl+Z on the source Part takes the extracted one away again, and redo brings it back. A preview you don't accept goes away as soon as you change the source Part or pick another one, and it is never saved.

Delete a Part#

Click Delete... (or right-click a row > Delete...) and confirm. The last Part can't be deleted, and a delete can't be undone.

Parts in files#

FormatWhat happens to Parts
Project (.sfproj)Every Part is saved with its name, visibility, folder, attributes, layers and active layer; linked copies keep their link
OBJEach visible Part is an o object; on import, o objects become Parts and g groups become face groups
glTF / GLBOne node and mesh per visible Part, both ways
FBXOne model per visible Part (with LODs, an LOD group each), both ways
USDOne Mesh prim per visible Part, both ways
BridgeEach visible Part is sent as its own object
PLY, STL, splat conversionThe visible Parts are merged into one mesh

Opening a multi-object file replaces the scene with one Part per object. See Exporting for the details of each format.

Scripting#

Every Part action is a command, so you can use them in the console, scripts, Python and MCP agents. Parts are addressed by index (0 is the top of the list) or name; without either, the active one is used.

CommandWhat it does
part_listList the Parts
part_select index=1Make a Part active
part_append type=cubeAdd a primitive (sphere, cube, plane); path= adds a file; size= (0.05-2, default 0.35)
part_duplicateCopy a Part
part_duplicate_linked / part_make_uniqueA linked copy sharing the mesh / break the link
part_delete name=HelmetDelete a Part
part_rename to=BodyRename a Part
part_visibility visible=falseShow or hide (toggles when visible is left out)
part_solo / part_show_allSolo the active Part / show every Part
part_move direction=upMove a Part up or down the list
part_folder_new folder=EyesMake a folder holding a Part (empty=true: an empty one)
part_set_folder name=EyeL folder=EyesPut a Part into a folder (folder= empty: out of its folder)
part_folder_visibility folder=EyesHide or show a folder's Parts (toggles when visible is left out)
part_folder_collapse / part_folder_rename / part_folder_deleteFold or open, rename, remove a folder (folder=)
transform translate=0,0.1,0 folder=trueTransform the visible Parts in the active Part's folder
part_merge_down / part_merge_visibleMerge
part_split_groups / part_split_partsSplit
