Subdivision levels

Subdivision levels let you sculpt one model at several resolutions: block in the big forms on a light version of the mesh, add pores and wrinkles on a dense one, and go back down to change the silhouette without losing the detail. Every Part has its own levels.

Everything is in the Levels group at the top of Tool > Geometry, in the order you use it:

ControlShortcutWhat it does
Level sliderSteps to any level (1 is the base cage)
Lower LevelShift+DSteps one level down
Higher LevelDSteps one level up
Add LevelCtrl+DAdds a level above the highest one and steps to it
SmoothSmooths the next new level (on by default)
Rebuild Lower LevelsFinds a lower level for a mesh that was subdivided elsewhere
Remove BelowDeletes the levels below the current one (asks first)
Remove AboveDeletes the levels above the current one (asks first)

Under the buttons the palette says where you are ("Level 3 of 5") and how many triangles the base and the top have. The Part list shows the level of every Part that has them ("Level 3/5").

Add Level#

Add Level (Ctrl+D) adds a level with four times the polygons. The lower levels stay, so you can step back down at any time.

  • Quads stay quads. A mesh with quads is divided with Catmull-Clark subdivision: each quad becomes four quads, and a triangle among them becomes three quads. A mesh of triangles only (a sphere from New Sphere, a scan) is divided with Loop subdivision: each triangle becomes four.
  • Smooth (next to Add Level) smooths the new level, rounding off the shape. Turn it off to divide flat: the new level keeps the hard edges and flat faces of the one below, for hard-surface pieces.
  • Add Level always adds the level on top. If you are at a lower level it steps to the highest first.
  • Creased edges stay sharp. Edges creased in Geometry > Edges (Edges) on the base level keep a hard line through the levels: a crease of 1 stays sharp on every level, lower values round off after a few levels.
  • Mask, vertex paint, paint layers and UVs are interpolated onto the new level; face groups and hidden faces carry over; sculpt layers are divided with the shape.
  • How far you can divide depends on your computer's memory: about 160 million triangles with 32 GB (each triangle of a divided model takes about 90 bytes, plus some room while dividing). Add Level says so when the next level wouldn't fit.

Tip: Start from a light mesh. A cube of 108 triangles divided five times is a smooth 110,000-triangle head-sized blob; a 25,000-triangle base divided four times is already 6.5 million.

Stepping between levels#

Press Shift+D to step down and D to step up, or drag the Level slider. Each step is instant once you have visited a level; the first visit after a big edit lower down takes a moment on millions of triangles.

What happens to your sculpting when you change level:

  • Detail survives. Each level stores its detail as a small offset from the smoothed shape of the level below, measured along that surface (its normal and tangents). Stepping up and down without sculpting gives back exactly the same mesh.
  • Big changes low, detail follows. Sculpt, move or inflate at a low level and step up: the higher levels follow the new shape and the wrinkles, pores and scales you sculpted on them ride along on top.
  • Detail edits show up below. Sculpt at a high level and step down: the lower levels take on a softened version of the change, so the silhouette stays honest whichever level you look at. Fine detail is too small for a low level and only shows as a gentle swelling there.

What follows the levels#

When you change it at one level
ShapeCarried up as detail, softened down (see above)
Sculpt layersEach layer has its own detail on every level, kept relative to the surface like the shape's: rotate or reshape a low level and a layer's wrinkles turn and bend with it. Weights apply to all levels. See Layers and subdivision levels
MaskCopied down to the vertices below, interpolated up where it changed
Vertex Paint and paint layersAs the mask: paint at a low level for broad colour, at a high level for detail. Painting low only changes the higher levels where you painted
Face Groups, hidden facesCopied down to the parent faces, inherited up by the faces they contain
UVsCopied down and interpolated up
TextureShared by every level (it lives on the UVs)
Edge creasesSet on the base level; the levels above are made with them. Creasing or uncreasing at Level 1 reshapes the higher levels, their sculpted detail riding along

Clearing the mask, removing a layer or deleting the paint layers does it on every level.

Remove Below and Remove Above#

Remove Below deletes the levels below the current one: the current level becomes the new base cage (Level 1). What is left of the edge creases from the deleted levels becomes the new base's own creases, so the levels above keep their shape. Remove Above deletes the levels above, and the detail sculpted on them. Both are undoable. Deleting down to a single level leaves an ordinary mesh.

Rebuild Lower Levels#

A model that was subdivided in another application (or exported from SculptForge at its top level) comes in as one dense mesh. Rebuild Lower Levels finds the mesh it was subdivided from and adds it as a new lowest level, keeping the dense mesh exactly as it was. Press it again for more levels.

  • It works at the lowest level (Level 1).
  • The mesh must be a regular subdivision: all quads from Catmull-Clark subdivision, or all triangles from Loop subdivision. Otherwise it says so and leaves the mesh alone.
  • The vertices of the new lower level sit on the dense mesh's corner vertices.

Tip: The Cube from Tool > Parts > Append is a regular quad grid, so Rebuild Lower Levels takes it down to a 3 x 3 cube in four steps.

Undo, projects and exporting#

  • Undo (Ctrl+Z) is one step per stroke at any level, and one step per level change, so undo walks back through your strokes and level changes in the order you made them. Add Level, Remove Below, Remove Above and Rebuild Lower Levels are single undo steps. Steps share the levels they didn't change, so they cost little memory.
  • Projects (.sfproj) save every level of every Part and open at the level you saved at. They use project format 5 (sculpt-layer detail relative to the surface), so older SculptForge versions ask you to update before opening them. Projects saved with format 4 open as before and are converted as they load.
  • Exports and Bridge write the current level. To write another one, step to it first, or use the subdiv_export command (subdiv_export path=base.obj level=1 writes the base cage of every visible Part, in any export format). LODs and Send to Unreal start from the current level too.
  • Tools that rebuild the mesh (Voxel Remesh, Decimate, Quad Remesh, Subdivide, Remove Hidden, merging and splitting Parts) work on the level you are on, including what you just sculpted there, and make a new mesh without levels: that level becomes the new base. The status line says so, e.g. Subdivision levels removed: Level 3 of 5 kept as the new base (undo restores them). Ctrl+Z brings every level back, at the level you were on. Merging and splitting Parts can't be undone, so step to the level you want to keep first, or save before.

Note: Subdivide (removes levels) (in Tool > Geometry, under Resolution (no levels), apart from Add Level and Micro) replaces the mesh with a denser one and keeps no levels. On a Part with levels it asks first ("This removes your N subdivision levels"); Don't ask again turns that off. Remove Below and Remove Above ask too. Use Add Level unless you want exactly that.

Commands#

Every control is a command you can run in the console, from a script or over MCP (see Scripting):

CommandWhat it does
subdiv_divide smooth=trueAdd Level; smooth=false divides flat (Smooth off)
subdiv_level level=2Step to a level (1 = base)
subdiv_lower, subdiv_higherLower Level, Higher Level
subdiv_delete_lower, subdiv_delete_higherRemove Below, Remove Above
subdiv_reconstructRebuild Lower Levels
subdiv_infoList the levels and their triangle counts
subdiv_export path=base.obj level=1Export the visible Parts at a level

Speed and memory#

Measured on an Apple M1 Pro (Asahi Linux), a cube divided four times to 6.5 million triangles:

1.6M triangles at the top6.5M triangles at the top
Add Level to the top0.4 s4.3 s
Step down / up between visited levels1-4 ms0.1-45 ms
Step down right after sculpting the top0.05-0.13 s0.2-0.6 s
Step up after reshaping the base0.05-0.15 s0.6 s
Memory for all levels120 MB480 MB

SculptForge keeps the two levels you visited last ready to show, so going back and forth between a low and a high level (the usual rhythm) is instant. Only the active Part keeps them: selecting another Part frees that memory (50-170 MB for a Part with 1.8 million triangles at the top), and the first level change after you come back takes a moment longer (about 0.1 s at that size) while the level is prepared again.

Past the highest level#

For more detail than the computer can hold as a mesh (hundreds of millions of polygons), add a micro level on top of the highest level: see Micro levels.
