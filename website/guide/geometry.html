Subdivide, remesh, decimate and LODs

These tools change the topology of the active Part: add resolution for detail, rebuild an even surface, reduce the triangle count, make it symmetric, or make a chain of levels of detail for a game engine. This chapter explains what each one does to your mesh and its attributes, and when to use it.

All of them work on the active Part and, except the LOD preview, are undoable with Ctrl+Z.

Quads and triangles#

SculptForge sculpts on triangles, but it remembers which pairs of triangles were one quad. A model you open from ZBrush (Send to ZBrush), Blender or Maya (OBJ, FBX, PLY, USD) keeps its quads: Wire draws them as quads, without the diagonal, and OBJ, FBX, PLY, USD and GoZ-compatible exports write them back as quads, corner for corner. Polygons with more than four sides are split into triangles. The new Plane and Cube are made of quads, and so is the result of a voxel remesh.

Sculpting, masking, vertex paint, sculpt layers, undo, Parts and project files all keep the quads. Subdivide turns every quad into four quads. Two things turn quads into triangles:

  • Decimate (and the lower levels of a LOD chain) collapses edges, so its result is all triangles.
  • When the two halves of a quad end up in different face groups, or one half is deleted, they show and export as two triangles.

glTF (.glb) and STL only hold triangles, so those exports always write triangles.

ZBrush: Besides keeping the quads you bring in, SculptForge can make new ones from any mesh: Quad Remesh (like ZRemesher) builds quads with flowing edge loops. See Quad retopology.

Subdivide#

For sculpting at several resolutions use Divide (Ctrl+D), which keeps the lower levels to step back to: see Subdivision levels. Subdivide is the one-way version.

Tool > Geometry > Subdivide smooths the mesh with Loop subdivision. Each level multiplies the triangle count by four and rounds off the shape slightly, like ZBrush's Divide with Smt on.

  • Sculpt layers are subdivided with the mesh, so blend shapes keep working.
  • Mask, vertex paint and UVs are interpolated; face groups and hidden faces carry over.
  • Each quad becomes four quads; triangles become four triangles.
  • SculptForge refuses to subdivide past 50 million triangles.

Note: Subdivide replaces the mesh with a denser one and drops any subdivision levels: it subdivides the level you are on, which becomes the new base, and the status line says so (undo brings the levels back). Voxel Remesh, Decimate and Quad Remesh do the same. To add triangles only where you sculpt, use Dynamic topology.

Tip: The bar along the top shows the triangle count of the whole scene. A few million triangles sculpt smoothly on a recent GPU; go up a level only when you need finer detail.

Voxel remesh#

Tool > Remesh > Voxel Remesh rebuilds the active Part as an even, watertight surface, like ZBrush's DynaMesh. Use it to:

  • fuse overlapping pieces into one surface (append a sphere, overlap it, merge, remesh);
  • even out stretched triangles after heavy Move or Tendril work;
  • clean up scans, splat conversions and AI-generated models before sculpting.

The Resolution slider next to the button sets how many voxels span the longest side of the model (64 to 400 in the palette, default 200). Higher keeps more detail and makes more triangles.

What happens to your data:

  • Sculpt layers are baked into the surface: the result has no layers.
  • Mask and vertex paint are transferred from the nearest original vertex.
  • Face Groups, hidden faces and UVs are reset (everything becomes visible, one face group). Hidden parts are remeshed too.
  • A painted texture is kept with Keep Texture (on by default, shown next to the button when the Part has a texture): the result gets Auto UVs and the texture is projected onto it, every layer included, in the same undo step. This runs in the background (Auto UV of a few hundred thousand triangles takes several seconds) with a progress bar and Cancel. Off: the texture is dropped. See Texture painting.
  • Separate pieces that don't touch stay separate pieces; overlapping ones are merged.
  • The new surface is made of even quads (the original's quads are rebuilt, not kept).
  • Keep Masked (off by default): masked areas keep their triangles exactly, detail included, and only the rest is remeshed. The new surface is joined to the masked area along the mask's edge by a narrow strip of triangles, so the model stays closed. A triangle counts as masked when all three of its corners are at least half masked. A ragged mask edge is tidied by itself (#67): where it touches itself the corner triangles are remeshed, masked spots smaller than the new polygons are remeshed with the rest, and narrow gaps between masked areas are kept; the status line says when it did. If it still can't be joined, the status line says what to change (blur or grow the mask, or unmask where two parts overlap) and nothing changes. Command: remesh keep_masked=true.

To remesh several Parts into one, first combine them with Merge visible... or Merge down... in Parts.

ZBrush: There is no Project option, Groups-aware remesh or Blur slider yet. Like ZBrush's DynaMesh, the remesh produces an even grid of quads, not flowing edge loops. For quads with edge loops that follow the forms, use Quad Remesh (like ZRemesher), and Project All to get the detail back.

Quad Remesh#

Tool > Geometry > Quad Remesh is automatic quad retopology, like ZBrush's ZRemesher: it rebuilds the Part as clean quads whose edge loops follow the forms, close to a target polygon count, with Keep Groups, Adaptive Size, vertex paint density and symmetry. Project All (Tool > Parts > Project) brings the sculpted detail back onto the new topology after you divide it. Both have their own chapter: Quad retopology and detail projection.

Decimate#

Tool > Geometry > Decimate reduces the triangle count while keeping the shape, using quadric edge collapse (the same family of algorithm as ZBrush's Decimation Master). The Keep slider next to the button sets the fraction of triangles to keep (0.05 to 0.95, default 0.50).

Decimation keeps open borders in place, and because it only removes vertices, every remaining vertex keeps its exact sculpt layer offsets, mask and vertex paint. Face Groups, hidden flags and UVs survive on the remaining triangles. The result is all triangles: quads are not kept.

With Keep Masked (under the button, off by default) masked vertices are never removed, so masked areas keep their triangles exactly and only the rest is decimated (decimate keep_masked=true).

Use it to make a lighter version of a finished sculpt for export, 3D printing or a review, or to bring a dense scan down to something you can work with.

Tip: Decimate a duplicate Part (Tool > Parts > Duplicate) when you want to keep the full-resolution sculpt in the same project.

Mirror And Weld#

Mirror And Weld, in Tool > Geometry > Modify Topology, makes a model symmetric: it keeps the half on the

  • side of the chosen axis (+X is the model's left, your right when you look at its face), mirrors it onto the other side and welds the two halves along the middle into one watertight mesh. Use it after sculpting one side without symmetry, to finish a half model, or to repair a model whose sides have drifted apart.
  • Pick the axis with X, Y and Z beside the button (X is on by default). With several on, they apply one after the other.
  • The mirror plane goes through the world's origin, the same plane symmetry uses. Polygons that cross it are cut on it, so the seam is clean wherever the model sits. Points on an open edge within 0.1% of the model's size from the plane are welded onto it, which closes a half model whose seam isn't exactly at 0.
  • Face Groups, vertex paint, the mask, UVs, quads and sculpt layers are kept; the mirrored side takes the kept side's. Subdivision levels are removed (undo restores them), as with the other tools on this page.
  • To keep the other side, flip the model first with Tool > Deformation > Mirror, or run mirror_and_weld side=negative.

Mirror#

Tool > Deformation > Mirror flips the Part across the plane through the origin on the chosen axes, with its surface still facing outward. Face Groups, vertex paint, UVs and layers come along; subdivision levels are removed (undo restores them). mirror pivot=center flips it in place about its own centre instead.

LOD chains#

A level-of-detail chain is a series of lighter copies of the model for game engines: LOD0 is the full sculpt, and each level after it has a fixed fraction of the triangles of the one before.

  1. Open Tool > Export.
  2. Set LODs (2 to 6 levels, including LOD0) and Reduction (the triangle ratio from one level to the next, 0.1 to 0.9, default 0.50).
  3. Click Generate LOD chain.

A table lists each level with its triangle count and its budget as a percentage of LOD0. Click a level to preview it on the canvas (wireframe turns on and sculpting pauses); click Sculpt in the table to go back to the editable mesh. The next stroke, or a change to LODs or Reduction, clears the chain; click Generate LOD chain again to rebuild it.

Blend shapes (sculpt layers) are kept on every level, so morph targets work at every LOD.

To write the chain to disk:

  • LOD chain (GLB per level) in the same sub-palette, or File > Export LOD Chain..., writes one GLB per level named <name>_LOD0.glb, <name>_LOD1.glb and so on.
  • Send to Unreal and Export FBX for Unreal... pack the LODs into one FBX for Unreal's automatic LOD import.

See Exporting for these exports and their options.

Note: The preview in Tool > Export is of the active Part. The exports themselves build a chain for every visible Part.

Undo and safety#

Subdivide, remesh, Quad Remesh, decimate, Mirror And Weld, Mirror and delete hidden are single undo steps on the active Part. They can use a lot of memory on dense meshes, and undo keeps a full copy of the previous mesh for each of them. Before a big change on a heavy sculpt:

  • Snapshot (File > Save Snapshot) keeps a timestamped copy of the scene without touching your project file; see Projects.
  • Duplicate the Part to keep the old version side by side.

Scripting#

CommandOptions
subdividelevels 1-4 (default 1)
remeshresolution 32-512 voxels across the longest side (default 200); keep_texture (default true)
decimateratio 0.01-1 of the triangles to keep (default 0.5), or triangles for an exact target
qremeshtarget quads (default 5000), adaptive, keep_groups, detect_edges, use_polypaint, symmetry, keep_original, keep_texture; see Quad retopology
mirror_and_weldaxis x, y, z or several, e.g. xz (default x); side positive (default) or negative
mirroraxis (default x); pivot origin (default) or center
export_lodspath (base name), levels 1-8 including LOD0 (default 4), ratio 0.1-0.9 (default 0.5)
sculptforge-cli -c 'open path=scan.obj; remesh resolution=256; decimate triangles=200000; export_lods path=out/rock.glb levels=4 ratio=0.5'

The command line accepts a wider remesh range (32-512) than the palette slider.
