Masking

A mask protects part of the model from change. Masked areas are drawn darker, and every brush, the Paint brush and colour fills leave them alone. This chapter covers painting masks with Ctrl, masking with a lasso or rectangle, the background gestures, and the Tool > Masking controls for inverting, blurring, growing and building masks from cavities, face groups or the mesh border.

How masks work#

  • A mask is a value per vertex from 0 (free) to 1 (fully masked). Values in between give partial protection, so a soft mask edge blends sculpting into the protected area.
  • Masks belong to a Part: each Part has its own mask.
  • Masks are saved in projects (.sfproj) and in .GoZ files, so they survive a round trip to ZBrush.
  • Masking is not a layer and isn't affected by sculpt layers: a mask stroke always works on the model itself.
  • Mask strokes and mask commands are undoable like any other change.

To see the mask, keep Mask on in the right shelf (it is on by default; Ctrl+H toggles it, as ZBrush's View Mask). The same toggle is Show mask in Tool > Masking, and Show Mask in the Render palette. Turning it off hides the darkening without clearing the mask: it still protects the surface, the status bar says so, and turning it on again shows the same mask. Clear Mask (Ctrl+Shift+A) is what removes it.

Paint a mask#

  1. Hold Ctrl and drag on the model. The area under the brush becomes masked.
  2. Hold Ctrl+Alt and drag to erase the mask.

Mask strokes use the current Draw Size, symmetry and, with a pen, pressure (when Pressure > Strength is on). How much each stroke masks is set by Mask Strength in Tool > Masking (0.01 to 1, default 1): lower values build up a soft, partial mask over several strokes. Mask strokes always use a round tip, whatever alpha you have selected, and a Drag Stamp stroke masks with dabs along the path instead of one stamp.

On macOS, Cmd works wherever this guide says Ctrl.

ZBrush: this is ZBrush's Ctrl-masking with its MaskPen brush, the default. Mask Lasso and Mask Rectangle are described next.

Mask with a lasso or rectangle#

To mask a large or sharply outlined area in one go, pick a mask selection shape. As in ZBrush, holding Ctrl turns the brush on the left shelf into the mask brush: with Ctrl held, click the brush tile (or press B) to pick Mask Pen, Mask Lasso or Mask Rectangle, or click the stroke tile to pick the stroke (Dots, Lasso or Rect). The same shapes sit at the top of Tool > Masking:

ShapeCtrl + drag
Mask Pen (default)Paints the mask with the brush, as described above.
Mask LassoDraws a free-hand outline that closes back to where you started. Everything inside is masked.
Mask RectangleDraws a rectangle. Everything inside is masked.

With Mask Lasso or Mask Rectangle:

  1. Hold Ctrl and drag around the area. The drag can start on the model or on the background. The shape is drawn as you drag, white with a dark veil.
  2. Let go to mask what's inside. Hold Ctrl+Alt instead to unmask what's inside (the shape is drawn blue). You can press or release Alt while dragging; what counts is whether it is held when you let go.

The selection works in screen space, like the visibility rectangle: a vertex counts as inside when it falls inside the shape as you see it, including vertices on the far side of the model. Hidden parts are left alone. With symmetry on (X, Y, Z on the top shelf), the mirror image of what's inside is masked too, as a mask stroke mirrors. A shape that covers none of the model clears the mask instead (as in ZBrush). Mask Strength sets how much a selection masks or unmasks, and each selection is one undo step. A lasso that crosses itself leaves out the areas it encloses twice.

Turn on Front faces only (under the shape buttons, shown for Mask Lasso and Mask Rectangle) to mask only what faces you: the far side of the model and folds turned away from you are left as they are. It follows the mirror image too when symmetry is on.

The cursor turns into a small cross while Ctrl is held, since the brush size doesn't matter for a selection.

ZBrush: these are ZBrush's MaskLasso and MaskRect brushes. Like ZBrush, the shape stays selected until you pick another one, so switch back to Mask Pen to paint masks again. Holding Ctrl shows the mask brush on the shelf and Ctrl with the Brush or Stroke picker picks it, as in ZBrush. MaskCurve is not available yet.

Background gestures#

With Ctrl held, clicks and drags on the empty background act on the whole mask:

GestureEffect
Ctrl + click the backgroundInvert the mask
Ctrl + drag on the backgroundDraw a mask rectangle (Mask Pen; Mask Lasso draws a lasso): what it covers is masked, mirrored by the symmetry. A shape that covers none of the model clears the mask.
Ctrl + drag on the background, with nothing maskedZoom the view in the SculptForge style (the default); with the ZBrush-style keys it draws the mask rectangle above (see Keyboard and mouse)

ZBrush: with DynaMesh on, ZBrush also remeshes when a Ctrl + drag on the background misses the model. SculptForge doesn't: remesh with Tool > Remesh instead.

On the model, a Ctrl + click that doesn't drag works on the mask's edges, as in ZBrush, whichever mask shape is picked:

GestureEffect
Ctrl + click the modelBlur the mask (once per click; same as Blur)
Ctrl+Alt + click the modelSharpen the mask (same as Sharpen)

Mask keys#

KeyEffect
Ctrl+AMask All
Ctrl+Shift+AClear Mask
Ctrl+IInvert Mask
Ctrl+HShow or hide the mask's shading (View Mask)
Ctrl+WGroup Masked: the masked part becomes a new face group and the mask is cleared (see Face Groups)

A drag starts after the cursor moves a few pixels, so a slightly shaky click still inverts. With Mask Lasso or Mask Rectangle, a Ctrl + drag on the background draws that shape; to clear the mask, draw it away from the model, or use Clear.

The Masking sub-palette#

Open Tool > Masking (the Tool palette is in the right tray by default). Every button is one undo step.

Mask operations only change visible parts of the model; hidden parts keep their mask. Clear is the exception and frees everything.

Common tasks#

Protect detail while smoothing#

  1. Click By cavity to mask the creases and pores.
  2. Smooth with Shift. The cavities keep their sharpness while the surface between them relaxes.
  3. Ctrl + drag on the background to clear the mask when you're done.

Raise threshold if too much is masked; lower Mask Strength for partial protection.

Sculpt one part only#

  1. Paint a mask over the part you want to work on with Ctrl.
  2. Ctrl + click the background to invert it. Now everything except that part is protected.
  3. Sculpt freely; strokes that cross the edge stop at the mask.

Mask a whole face group#

The quickest way works without knowing the group's number:

  1. Ctrl+Shift + click the group to isolate it (see Face Groups and visibility).
  2. Click All: only visible vertices are masked.
  3. Ctrl+Shift + click the background to show everything again. The mask stays.

By face group masks a group by its id. The viewport doesn't show ids, but the console does: isolate the group, then open the console (`) and read the isolate_polygroup group=N line. Enter that number in the field next to By face group and click it.

Sculpt without breaking the border#

A plane, a cloth panel or a part cut from a larger model has an open border that should stay where it is, for example to keep it lined up with the piece it joins:

  1. Set Rings to how wide the protected strip should be.
  2. Click By border. The open edges and the rings inside them are masked.
  3. Sculpt freely; the border doesn't move. Tick Soft first for a gradual transition into the sculpted area.

Tick Face Group borders to also protect the seams between face groups. This is Blender's Mask from mesh boundary (and, with face sets, Mask from face set boundary).

A soft-edged mask#

Paint the mask, then click Blur a few times (or set passes to 3-5 first). Sharpen does the opposite.

Turn a mask into a face group#

Click From mask in Tool > Face Groups: the masked triangles become a new face group. See Face Groups and visibility.

Masks and painting#

Vertex Paint respects the mask too: the Paint brush, Rgb strokes and Fill leave masked areas alone, so you can mask an area and fill the rest with a colour. See Vertex Paint and materials.

Masking from a script#

CommandOptions
mask_clear, mask_all, mask_invert
mask_blur, mask_sharpen, mask_grow, mask_shrinkiterations (1-100, default 1)
mask_by_cavitystrength (0-1, default 1), threshold (-1 to 1, default 0.02)
mask_by_polygroupgroup (required), add=true to add to the current mask instead of replacing it
mask_borderrings (1-20, default 1), polygroups=true to also mask face group borders, soft=true to fade inwards, strength (0-1, default 1), add=true to add to the current mask
mask_rectx0 y0 x1 y1 (-1 to 1, y up), unmask, strength (0-1, default 1), optional view_proj matrix; without a view the rectangle spans the mesh seen from the front
mask_lassopoints (required): the outline as "x0 y0 x1 y1 ...", at least 3 points in the same -1 to 1 space; unmask, strength, view_proj as for mask_rect
sculptforge-cli -c 'open path=head.obj; mask_by_cavity threshold=0.05; mask_blur iterations=2; save_project path=head.sfproj'

Not yet: masking by topology, by normals or by alpha, Mask By Smoothness, and MaskCurve are not available yet.
