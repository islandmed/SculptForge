Baking normal and displacement maps

Game engines and renderers want light meshes. Baking captures the detail of your high-poly sculpt as images laid over the UVs of a low-poly version: a normal map makes the low-poly shade as if it had every crease and pore, a displacement map stores how far the surface moves, and ambient occlusion, cavity and colour maps feed materials. Tool > Bake makes all of them in one go, in the background, and shows the normal map on the low-poly right away so you can compare it with the sculpt.

What you need#

  • A high-poly source: one or more Parts with the detail. They stay as they are.
  • A low-poly Part with UVs, in the same place as the high-poly. It can come from another app (import it with Tool > Parts > Append; models with UVs keep them), or you make it in SculptForge: Duplicate the sculpt, Tool > Geometry > Decimate it, then Tool > Texture Map > Auto UV.

The maps go onto the active Part, so select the low-poly before baking.

Bake in five steps#

  1. Select the low-poly Part and open Tool > Bake in the right tray. The first line says which Part the maps go onto, or "No UVs" if it needs Auto UV first.
  2. Source: leave All visible Parts to bake from everything that is shown (except the low-poly), or pick one Part.
  3. Tick the maps you want, and choose the size (1K, 2K, 4K or 8K).
  4. Click Bake. A progress bar shows while it works on every processor core; you can keep working, or click Cancel Bake.
  5. When it is done, the status line reports what was baked and where the files went, and the low-poly shows its normal map. Hide the high-poly (Tool > Parts, the eye) to compare, or untick Preview Normal Map to see the bare low-poly.

The maps#

MapFileWhat it holds
Normal (tangent)<name>_normal.pngThe high-poly's surface direction relative to the low-poly, in tangent space. The map for game engines
Normal (object / world)<name>_normal_object.pngThe high-poly's normals in model space (Parts have no transform of their own, so this is also the world normal). For tools that want object-space maps
Displacement<name>_displacement.png and .exrThe distance from the low-poly to the high-poly along the low-poly's normal
Ambient Occlusion<name>_ao.pngHow much of the sky each point sees: darker in creases, folds and cavities
Cavity<name>_cavity.pngThe fine surface curvature: mid-grey where the surface is smooth, dark in cracks, light on ridges
Colour<name>_color.pngThe source's vertex paint, or its painted texture
Curvature<name>_curvature.pngHow the surface bends: mid-grey flat, brighter on convex edges and ridges, darker in creases. A bend of the curvature radius (2% of the model's size by default) is fully bright or dark. Drives edge wear
Thickness<name>_thickness.pngHow far rays travel through the high-poly before leaving it: dark where it is thin (ears, fins, blades). Drives translucency
Position<name>_position.png (16-bit)Each point's place in the bounding box, as red, green and blue. Drives gradients and noise without UV seams
ID<name>_id.pngA flat colour per face group (or per Part, id=parts), to pick areas in other programs

<name> is the low-poly Part's name. Colour is skipped when the source has neither vertex paint nor a texture.

Bake from the Part's own levels#

A Part with subdivision levels can bake its sculpted detail onto itself: choose Own top subdivision level as the Source, and the top level is baked onto the lowest level's UVs (give the lowest level UVs first). A Part with a micro level offers Own micro level, baked onto its base. No second Part, no matching: the levels share their UVs.

UDIM tiles#

When the low-poly's UVs go beyond the 0..1 square (UDIM tiles, for example from the UV Editor), every tile bakes as its own set of maps: <name>_normal.1001.png, <name>_normal.1002.png, ... (1001 + u + 10 v, the usual numbering).

The same maps, baked on the texture's own UVs, feed Fill layers inside SculptForge.

Tangent-space normal maps that look right everywhere#

SculptForge computes tangents with MikkTSpace, the same method Blender, Unreal Engine, Unity, Godot, Substance and xNormal use. A map baked here shows correctly in all of them, as long as the low-poly is exported with its normals and UVs (every SculptForge export keeps them). Choose the green channel convention under the map settings:

  • OpenGL (Y+): Blender, Unity, Godot, Substance, three.js. The default.
  • DirectX (Y-): Unreal Engine's native convention. Send to Unreal flips an OpenGL map on import (see below), so you only need DirectX when you import into Unreal by hand.

Displacement: mid-grey is zero#

The 16-bit PNG stores zero displacement as mid-grey, the high-poly outside the low-poly as lighter and inside as darker. White means +scale and black -scale in world units; the scale is the largest distance found, shown under the Bake button ("Displacement: mid-grey = 0, white = +0.0143") and in the status line. Use it as the displacement strength in your renderer (with a midlevel of 0.5). With EXR displacement ticked, a 32-bit float OpenEXR holds the real distances in world units, with no scale to set.

Settings#

SettingWhat it does
SourceThe high-poly: one Part, or every visible Part except the active one. Hidden faces of the source are ignored
Size1K, 2K (default), 4K or 8K square
AAAnti-aliasing: 1 to 4 means 1, 4, 9 or 16 rays per texel, averaged. 2 (the default) gives clean edges
Auto distanceMeasure how far the high-poly lies in front of and behind the low-poly, and cast rays just that far. After a bake, Front and Rear show what it measured
Front / RearWith Auto off: how far in front of the low-poly rays start, and how far behind it they look, as a percentage of the model's size (its bounding-box diagonal). ZBrush and Substance call these the max frontal and rear distances
PaddingTexels around each UV island filled with the island's edge colours, so seams don't show once an engine mipmaps the map. 8 suits 2K; use 16 for 4K and 8K
OpenGL / DirectXThe normal map's green channel convention (see above)
AO rays, AO reachAmbient occlusion quality, and how far away geometry still darkens a point (a percentage of the model's size)
Output folderWhere the files go. Empty: beside your last export, else the project's folder, else Bakes in your library folder. Open Folder shows it after a bake
EXR displacementAlso write the displacement as a 32-bit float EXR

How the bake works#

For every texel of the low-poly's UVs, SculptForge finds the matching point on the low-poly and casts a ray along its smoothed normal, from Front outside the surface to Rear inside it (a "cage"), and keeps the high-poly hit nearest to the low-poly. The normal, distance, occlusion and colour found there go into the maps. Rays follow the smoothed normals, so hard edges of the low-poly don't leave gaps.

  • Missing detail ("x% of texels found no surface" in red under the Bake button): rays didn't reach the high-poly. Turn off Auto distance and raise Front and Rear.
  • Detail from the wrong place (for example the inside of an ear showing on its back): the rays reach too far. Lower Rear (or Front), or bake the parts from separate Parts with Source.
  • The bake works on copies, so you can keep sculpting meanwhile. If the low-poly's triangles or UVs change afterwards, the normal map preview turns off because it no longer fits (undo brings it back).

Tip: Bake time grows with the map area and the number of rays. On a 10-core Apple M1 Pro, from a 1.3-million-triangle sculpt: a 4K tangent normal map plus displacement (AA 2) takes about 4 seconds, the same at 8K about 11 seconds. Ambient occlusion costs the most: adding it at 4K with 48 rays per texel brings the bake to about 30 seconds. Try settings at 1K, then bake the final maps at 4K.

Send to Unreal with the normal map#

When the last bake made a normal map, its low-poly Part is shown and still fits the map, Send to Unreal (Tool > Export) imports the map too, as T_<Name>_N next to the mesh, set up as a normal map (linear, normal map compression), with Unreal's Flip Green Channel set for an OpenGL map. This works with an Unreal Editor on the same computer; for one on another computer, import the PNG by hand. See Exporting, Unreal and ZBrush.

Commands#

CommandWhat it does
bakeBake onto the active Part and write the maps. Options: source=all, a Part name, subdivision or micro, maps=normal,object_normal,displacement,ao,cavity,color,curvature,thickness,position,id (or all), udim=true, thickness_rays=24, thickness_distance=0.1, curvature_radius=0.02, id=polygroups or parts, size=2048 (512 to 8192, or 1k...8k), out=folder, name=prefix, front=0.02 and rear=0.02 (fractions of the model's size, or auto), samples=2, padding=8, ao_rays=48, ao_distance=0.05, green=opengl or directx, displacement_scale= (world units at white), exr=true

For example, from the console or sculptforge-cli:

subtool_select name=Body_low; bake source=Body maps=normal,ao,displacement size=4096 out=maps

The command runs in the foreground (scripts wait for it); the Bake button runs the same bake in the background. See Scripting and automation.
