Vertex Paint and materials

Vertex Paint (ZBrush users: Polypaint) stores a colour on every vertex of the mesh, so you can paint a sculpt without UVs or textures. This chapter covers the Paint brush, painting while you sculpt, fills and cavity paint, picking colours, paint layers with blend modes, and the Lit Sphere materials that change how the model is shaded in the viewport.

How vertex paint works#

  • Colour is stored per vertex. The detail you can paint is limited by how dense the mesh is: on a light mesh, colour edges look soft. Subdivide or remesh at a higher resolution before fine painting.
  • Painting respects the mask (masked areas keep their colour) and skips hidden faces.
  • Symmetry works for painting as it does for sculpting.
  • Vertex Paint is per Part and is saved in projects. It is exported as vertex colours to PLY, glTF/GLB, OBJ, USD (displayColor) and GoZ-compatible files. FBX export doesn't include it.
  • A mesh starts without vertex paint. The first paint stroke or fill adds it (white everywhere you haven't painted).

Texture painting: to paint finer than the mesh, give the Part a texture and paint into it instead; the Paint brush, fills and paint layers work the same way. Vertex Paint > Texture bakes vertex paint into a texture. See Texture painting.

Paint with the Paint brush#

  1. Press P, click Paint on the top shelf, or pick Paint on the bottom shelf.
  2. Choose a colour (see below).
  3. Stroke over the model. Draw Size, the stroke type and the alpha work as they do for sculpting; RGB Intensity (I) sets how strongly the colour goes on.

While the Paint brush is active:

  • Alt paints back towards white.
  • Shift still smooths the shape, as in ZBrush.
  • The surface isn't changed.

Press P again, or click Sculpt on the top shelf, to go back to sculpting.

ZBrush: Paint is ZBrush's Rgb on with Zadd off. Sculpt with Rgb on is Rgb with Zadd: the brush sculpts and paints at once.

Paint while sculpting#

Turn on Rgb on the top shelf (or RGB while sculpting in the Paint menu). Every sculpt brush then also paints the current colour along its stroke. The Smooth brush and Shift-smoothing never paint.

Choose a colour#

  • The left shelf has a colour picker (a square plus a hue strip), two swatches and a Fill button. Clicking the second swatch swaps it with the main colour, like ZBrush's Alternate.
  • The Paint menu has a larger hue wheel with a hex value, ten preset swatches (white, near black, red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, skin and brown) and the fill buttons.
  • Press C with the cursor over the model to pick the colour under it (the mesh needs vertex paint for this, or a texture while painting into the texture).

Fill, cavity paint and clear#

The Paint menu has four buttons:

ButtonWhat it does
FillFills the unmasked area with the colour (the whole mesh when nothing is masked). Same as Fill on the left shelf
Fill maskedFills only the masked area. Available when the mesh has a mask
CavityDarkens the creases and cavities with a dark brown, respecting the mask. A quick way to bring out detail
ClearRemoves all vertex paint from the active Part

All of them are undoable. Show vertex paint in the same palette (or Paint on the right shelf) turns the colours off in the viewport without deleting them.

Tip: To paint a base colour and then dirt in the creases: Fill with the base colour, then Cavity. For a different cavity colour or strength, use the command (below).

Paint layers#

Paint layers stack vertex paint the way Photoshop stacks image layers. Each layer has its own name, visibility, opacity and blend mode, so you can paint dirt on a Multiply layer, glow on a Screen layer or tint on an Overlay layer, then change or hide it later without repainting the rest. Every Part has its own stack.

How paint layers work#

  • Base is the bottom layer: the Part's vertex paint, always opaque. With only Base, vertex paint works exactly as it did before, and nothing changes in your files.
  • Layers above Base start transparent. Painting on a layer adds colour and coverage: where you haven't painted, the layer is see-through. Painting never touches the layers you aren't on.
  • What you see, and what every export writes, is the stack composited from Base up: each visible layer blends its colour over the result below with its blend mode, scaled by its coverage and opacity.
  • Blending happens on display colours, like Photoshop, so the modes behave as Photoshop users expect: 50% grey on an Overlay or Soft Light layer changes nothing, white on Multiply changes nothing, black on Screen changes nothing.
  • The Paint brush, Rgb while sculpting, Fill, Fill masked and Cavity all write into the active layer (highlighted in the list; the Paint menu shows "Painting on: ..."). They respect the mask and hidden faces on every layer.
  • With the Paint brush on a layer, Alt erases the layer's paint (on Base it paints back towards white, as before).
  • A hidden or locked layer can't be painted, filled or cleared; the status line says why.

Blend modes#

ModeEffect
NormalThe layer's colour covers what is below
MultiplyDarkens: dirt, shadows, cavity grime. White does nothing
ScreenLightens: highlights, glow, dust. Black does nothing
OverlayAdds contrast and tints the midtones. 50% grey does nothing
Soft LightA gentler Overlay, good for skin tints and colour variation
AddAdds the colours (Photoshop's Linear Dodge); strong highlights
SubtractSubtracts the layer's colour
Darken / LightenKeeps the darker / lighter colour, channel by channel
DifferenceThe difference between the colours; useful for checking two paint passes against each other

The Paint Layers sub-palette#

Open Tool > Paint Layers in the right tray (it sits under the sculpt Layers sub-palette). The list shows the layers top first, with Base at the bottom, like Photoshop's Layers panel. Each row has the name, the blend mode and opacity, a lock and an eye.

  • Click a row to paint on that layer. Double-click it to rename. Right-click for Rename, Duplicate, Merge down, Clear and Delete.
  • The eye shows or hides the layer; the lock protects it from painting, fills, clears and merges.
  • Under the list: the active layer's blend mode and Opacity slider. The opacity previews live while you drag and is one undo step when you let go.
  • New adds a transparent layer above the active one. Duplicate copies the active layer (Base becomes an opaque layer). Delete removes the active layer.
  • The arrows move the active layer up or down. Merge down merges it into the layer below (into Base for the lowest layer). Flatten bakes every visible layer into Base and removes the layers; hidden layers are discarded.
  • Clear layer makes the active layer transparent again (Base: white). Fill layer fills it with the current colour, like Fill in the Paint menu.

Hold Ctrl over any control for its description. Every action is undoable with Ctrl+Z.

Tip: Merging onto Base, and merging a layer onto a Normal layer it fully covers, keeps the look exactly. Merging a Multiply, Overlay or other blended layer onto a partly transparent layer can't always match, so the result takes the lower layer's blend mode, as in Photoshop.

Paint layers and sculpt layers#

They are separate stacks. Tool > Layers holds sculpt layers: shape changes exported as blend shapes. Tool > Paint Layers holds colour. Selecting a sculpt layer doesn't change the paint layer you paint on, and the other way round, so a stroke with Rgb on can sculpt into the "Smile" layer and paint into the "Blush" layer at once.

Files, Parts and geometry#

  • Paint layers are saved in projects (.sfproj) with their names, blend modes, opacity, visibility, lock and the active layer. Older versions of SculptForge open these projects with the paint flattened.
  • Exports (OBJ, PLY, glTF/GLB, USD, GoZ-compatible) write the composited colours, exactly what the viewport shows.
  • Duplicating a Part copies its layers, and Split keeps them on each piece. Subdividing and decimating keep them too.
  • Merging Parts, a voxel remesh and other rebuilds keep the look but flatten the layers into Base.

ZBrush and Mudbox: ZBrush's polypaint layers only have opacity. Here every layer also has a blend mode, its own coverage and a lock, like the paint layers in Mudbox and Photoshop.

Materials (Lit Spheres)#

Materials change only how the model is shaded in the viewport. They aren't exported and don't change vertex paint.

MaterialLook
Soft ClayThe default: a matte warm light grey with a clear light and shadow side and a soft rim
SculptMade for sculpting: neutral warm clay under a strong key light from the upper left, a crisp highlight and deep cavity shading, so small strokes and undulations stay readable
Red WaxGlossy deep red wax, the classic sculpting look
ClayMatte grey-brown clay
SkinWarm, soft skin tones
ChromeMirror-like metal, good for checking surface flow
WhiteNeutral off-white for judging forms
BronzePolished bronze
StudioSofter clay shading with studio lighting (the default in earlier versions). Its colour is Base colour in Render > Materials

To choose one:

  • Press M for the material picker under the cursor, or click the Material tile on the left shelf. Point at a material to preview it on the model, click to keep it. Esc or clicking away puts the previous material back.
  • Open Render > Materials, or the Material tab of the Library.

Vertex Paint multiplies the material, and cavity shading stays on with every material (strongest with Sculpt). The material is saved with the project: projects saved with Studio open with Studio.

Colour tints the material (in the Paint menu, on by default): on a Part with no vertex paint yet, the current colour tints its Lit Sphere material, as in ZBrush, so you can try a skin, stone or bronze tone without painting anything. The colour's hue and saturation tint it; grey (the starting colour) and black leave the material as it is, and the material keeps its own light and shade. Once a Part has vertex paint, the paint shows instead. PBR shading uses the material channels and is not tinted. Saved with the project.

Tip: Sculpt is the material to judge strokes with. The prettier ones (Skin, Red Wax) soften small detail; switch to them for presenting.

Your own Lit Spheres#

A Lit Sphere is a picture of a lit sphere (Sloan and others, 2001): the view colours each point of the model with the sphere's colour where its surface faces the same way. Any lit-sphere image made for ZBrush, Blender or Nomad works: a square PNG, JPG or EXR with the sphere filling it (a non-square image is cropped to its centre; big ones are scaled to 1024 pixels).

  • Render > Materials > Load Lit Sphere... picks an image. It joins the material list under its file name, the view uses it, and a copy goes into the MatCaps folder of your library (Documents/SculptForge/MatCaps, or the folder set in Edit > Preferences > Library), so it is there next time.
  • Or put images into that MatCaps folder yourself: they show up the next time you open the material picker, Render > Materials or the Library's Material tab, no restart needed. Studios can share a read-only folder by listing it in the SCULPTFORGE_MATCAP_PATH environment variable (separated by :, ; on Windows); those load when SculptForge starts.
  • To remove one, right-click it in the material picker or palette and choose Remove. An image from your MatCaps folder goes to the trash (the Recycle Bin on Windows), so you can still get it back. On macOS it is only hidden until you restart, and the message says where its file is so you can delete it yourself. Built-in materials can't be removed.

Your Lit Spheres appear in the material picker (M), Render > Materials and the Library, after the built-in ones. A project that uses one carries a copy of the image, so it opens with the same look on another computer (or after you delete the file). Loading an image with the same name again replaces it. EXR images are read as linear light, so very bright highlights clip to white.

Smooth and flat shading#

Smooth shading (the default) blends the light across polygons, so the surface reads as one form. Flat shading lights every polygon on its own, so you see the faces: how the mesh is built, where its vertices sit, and exactly what a stroke, crease or pinch did to the geometry, without smooth blending hiding small lumps. It helps most when blocking out forms on a low-poly base mesh.

  • Render > Materials > Shading: Smooth | Flat for the whole scene. The same buttons are in Tool > Display.
  • This Part: Scene (the default) follows the scene's shading; Smooth or Flat keeps the active Part that way whatever the scene says, for example a flat body with smooth eyes.

Shading works with every material, vertex paint, textures and the mask. Like the material, it only changes the view: exports are unaffected. Projects save the scene's shading and each Part's own; projects from before open with your preferred shading. The scene's shading you choose is also the preference for the next session once you Store Config (Ctrl+Shift+I). Smooth and Flat have no default shortcut, so no ZBrush key changes meaning; assign one under Edit > Preferences > Hotkeys if you switch often.

Not yet: loading your own Lit Sphere images, and physically based materials or BPR-style renders.

Scripting#

CommandWhat it does
set_color color=#c08060Set the paint colour: rrggbb, #rrggbb (quote it in scripts), r,g,b or a name such as white, skin, bone
paint_fill area=unmaskedFill; area is unmasked (default), masked or all; strength 0-1
paint_from_cavity color=#202020 strength=0.7 smoothing=2Cavity paint (defaults: dark brown, 0.7, 2 blur passes)
paint_clearRemove all vertex paint, paint layers included
paint_layer_add name=Dirt blend=multiply opacity=0.8Add a paint layer above the active one and paint on it
paint_layer_set index=0 opacity=0.5 blend=overlay visible=false locked=true name=GrimeChange a layer (layer=Dirt picks it by name; default: the active layer)
paint_layer_select layer=BasePaint on another layer (index=-1 is Base, 0 the first layer above it)
paint_layer_duplicate, paint_layer_delete, paint_layer_clearCopy, delete or clear the active (or given) layer
paint_layer_move direction=upMove a layer up or down the stack
paint_layer_merge / paint_layer_flattenMerge down / bake every visible layer into Base
paint_layer_listList the layers, top first (* marks the active one)
set_material name=ChromeViewport material: a Lit Sphere name (Soft Clay is the default, your own Lit Spheres included) or studio (desktop app only)
load_matcap path=gold.pngLoad a Lit Sphere image and use it; keep=false doesn't copy it into the MatCaps folder (desktop app only)
remove_matcap name=GoldRemove one of your Lit Spheres; its image in the MatCaps folder goes to the trash (desktop app only)
set_background mode=flat color=60,70,80The view's background: gradient (the default) or flat with a colour (#rrggbb, r,g,b or a name); see The background (desktop app only)
set_shading mode=flatThe scene's shading: smooth (the default) or flat (desktop app only)
subtool_shading mode=smooth name=EyesA Part's own shading: default (follow the scene), smooth or flat; index or name picks the Part (default: the active one)
set_color color="#d9b38c"; paint_fill; paint_from_cavity strength=0.5
paint_layer_add name=Dirt blend=multiply opacity=0.8; paint_from_cavity color=4a2c18 strength=0.9
