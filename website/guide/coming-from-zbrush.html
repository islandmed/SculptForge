Coming from ZBrush

This chapter is for sculptors whose hands already know ZBrush. It lists what SculptForge calls the things you know, what works the same, what is different, and the shortcuts side by side, so you can start sculpting instead of relearning. SculptForge has its own keymap and offers ZBrush-style, Blender and Maya styles; turn on the ZBrush-style keys first (see ZBrush-style keys) and your hands will feel at home.

New installs start in SculptForge's own layout, with menus by task. For the alphabetical palette bar and the two-row top shelf you know, pick Coming from: ZBrush (keys and layout) or the ZBrush-style layout card in the welcome, or pick Edit > Preferences > Interface > Look > Layout > ZBrush-style (see Looks and layouts). Paths in this guide name the SculptForge layout's menus. In the ZBrush-style layout, leave out the menu: Edit > Preferences > Input is the palette named Preferences, Sculpt > Stroke the palette named Stroke; Tool > Parts is the same in both. See Menus and palettes for every palette's menu, and F3 finds any of them by name.

ZBrush-style keys#

New installs start with the SculptForge keys. The first time SculptForge starts it asks what you are Coming from: pick ZBrush and it turns on the ZBrush-style keys for you. To switch at any time, open Edit > Preferences > Input and choose ZBrush-style under Style, then press Store Config (Ctrl+Shift+I) to keep it. If you used an earlier version, your saved choice is kept.

What the ZBrush-style keys change:

ZBrush-styleSculptForge (default)
1 to 9, 0Free (9 is Save Snapshot, ZBrush's QuickSave)Pick the ten first brushes (Standard, Clay, Smooth, Move ...)
IRGB Intensity at the cursorIntensity at the cursor (as U)
Ctrl+drag on the background with nothing maskedDraws a mask rectangleZooms
Zoom drags (Ctrl+right-drag, Alt then release)Up zooms outUp zooms in

Everything else in this chapter works in both styles. Edit > Preferences > Input > Rotation chooses free rotation (as in ZBrush) or a turntable that keeps the model upright, as in Blender or Maya. Press Store Config to keep either. See Keyboard and mouse for the full list.

What's the same#

  • The layout (in the ZBrush-style layout). Shelves around the canvas, palettes in alphabetical order in the palette bar and the trays, sub-palettes that fold, the Library on ,, Ctrl-hover help on every control, Ctrl+Alt+click to assign a hotkey, Store Config on Ctrl+Shift+I. See The interface.
  • Navigation. Drag on the background to rotate freely (over the top, too), Alt+drag to move, Alt+click, let go of Alt and drag to scale, Shift during a rotation to snap to a side view, Shift+click, let go of Shift and drag to roll. Right-drag navigation works as with ZBrush's RightClick Navigation turned on, over the model too. F frames the model. See Navigating the view.
  • Sculpting modifiers. Alt swaps Add and Sub (ZBrush's Zadd and Zsub), Shift smooths (Smooth keeps its own Intensity; let go of Shift mid-stroke for the alternate smooth that keeps the volume), Shift pressed after a stroke starts draws a straight line.
  • Brush keys. B then two letters, with ZBrush's letters: B S T Standard, B C B Clay Block, B D S Sharp Crease, B M V Move, B I N Inflate, B S M Smooth, B S H Tendril, B P I Pinch.
  • Brush settings. S Draw Size, U Intensity, I RGB Intensity (ZBrush-style keys), O Falloff, each as a slider under the cursor; [ and ] step the size; Space opens the Quick Menu; L toggles Steady Stroke. The cursor's outer ring is the Draw Size, the inner ring the Falloff. See Sculpting and brushes.
  • Masking. Ctrl paints a mask and Ctrl+Alt erases it; Ctrl+click the model blurs it and Ctrl+Alt+click sharpens it; Ctrl+click the background inverts it and Ctrl+drag there draws a mask rectangle (ZBrush-style keys; one that misses the model clears the mask). Mask Lasso and Mask Rectangle follow the symmetry: with Ctrl held the shelf shows the mask brush, and the Brush (B) and Stroke pickers pick it. See Masking.
  • Visibility and face groups. Ctrl+Shift+drag keeps the inside (press Alt to hide it instead), Ctrl+Shift+click a group to isolate it (on a partly hidden mesh: to hide it), Ctrl+Shift+click the background to show everything, Ctrl+W to group the masked part. Select Rectangle and Select Lasso, also from the Brush and Stroke pickers with Ctrl+Shift held. Group by UVs is UV Groups. See Face Groups and visibility.
  • Parts with Alt+click to select one and Shift+Alt+click to select and frame it; subdivision levels with Ctrl+D, D and Shift+D; Wireframe on Shift+F; Save Snapshot on 9 (ZBrush-style keys). See Parts and Subdivision levels.
  • Send to ZBrush (GoZ-compatible). Send models to and from ZBrush. See Exporting, Unreal and ZBrush.
  • VDM brushes with Drag Stamp and Slide Stamp, and your own made from a VDM plane. See Vector displacement brushes.
  • Sculpting in Blender too? The Blender add-on can give Blender's Sculpt Mode ZBrush's keys. See Working with Blender.

Bring your ZTools over#

ZBrush's .ZTL files are proprietary, so SculptForge can't read them directly. The free SculptForge ZTL Export plug-in runs inside ZBrush and converts them once:

  1. Download SculptForgeZToolExport.txt (also attached to every release) and copy it into ZBrush's ZStartup/ZPlugs64 folder, then restart ZBrush. Or load it once with ZScript > Load.
  2. In ZBrush, ZPlugin > SculptForge > Convert ZTL asks for a .ZTL, loads it and writes each SubTool next to it as a GoZ-compatible file (Dragon.ZTL gives Dragon.sf001_Body.GoZ, Dragon.sf002_Horns.GoZ...). Save for SculptForge does the same for the ZTool you have open, saving the .ZTL where you choose.
  3. In SculptForge, open the .ZTL (File > Open, or drop it on the window). Its SubTools open as Parts in the same order with the same names, face groups, vertex paint, mask and UVs.

Each SubTool comes over at its highest subdivision level; the lower levels, sculpt layers and ZBrush materials don't. If you change the ZTool in ZBrush later, convert it again: SculptForge warns when the .ZTL is newer than its conversion. .ZPR projects can't be converted; save their ZTools first.

Note: the plug-in is new and hasn't been tested in every ZBrush version yet. If it stops with an error or a SubTool is missing, please report it with your ZBrush version; exporting each SubTool from ZBrush as a .GoZ or .OBJ file works in the meantime.
