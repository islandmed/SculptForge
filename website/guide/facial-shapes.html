Facial shapes and live face

Generate Facial Shapes gives a sculpted head the 52 blend shapes Apple ARKit and Google's MediaPipe use for face capture (eyeBlinkLeft, jawOpen, mouthSmileRight and the rest), fitted to that head's own proportions. They are ordinary sculpt layers: you fix any of them by sculpting, test them with expression presets, export them as morph targets to a rig, a game engine or Blender, and drive them live, so the character makes your expressions.

What you need#

  • A humanoid head (human or stylised) as the active Part, its face pointing at the front view (+Z) with Y up, as SculptForge's front view shows it. A bust with shoulders is fine.
  • Separate Parts for the eyes, teeth and tongue, if the sculpt has them (see Parts).
  • Nothing to download: the template head the shapes come from is built into SculptForge.

To try it on a ready-made head, type new_head in the console (`): a life-size human head with a mouth and eye sockets, and its eyes as a second Part.

Generate the shapes#

  1. Select the head Part and open Tool > Facial Shapes.
  2. Click Generate Facial Shapes. It takes a second or two (more on millions of vertices).
  3. The status line says how well the template fitted: the fit error (as a share of the head's height; under 1% is good) and how much of the template's face found the sculpt.
  4. Tool > Layers now lists the 52 shapes, all at weight 0, so the sculpt looks unchanged.

Generating again replaces the 52 layers (other layers stay). Undo brings back the previous ones, together with any fixes you had sculpted into them.

Options#

  • Symmetry: Auto makes each Left / Right pair exact mirror images when the sculpt is symmetric about X = 0; On always mirrors (a slightly asymmetric sculpt gets the average); Off keeps each side as fitted.
  • Parts too: also give the other Parts the shapes they need (below).

How it works#

SculptForge carries a template head with all 52 shapes: the ICT Face Model Light from ICT-FaceKit by the USC Institute for Creative Technologies, under the MIT licence (free for commercial work: studios can ship what it makes). Generating:

  1. Finds the face. Without landmarks it looks for the nose tip (the most forward point near the middle) and the head's height, then lines the template's face up with the sculpt's (rotation, scale and position).
  2. Wraps the template around the sculpt (non-rigid registration): the template is pulled onto the closest points of the sculpt, stiffly at first and then more and more loosely, so it takes the sculpt's shape without tearing.
  3. Transfers each shape with deformation transfer: each small piece of the template's surface moves in the shape the way it moves on the template, rebuilt on the fitted template. A wide face gets a wide smile, a long jaw a long jaw drop.
  4. Binds the sculpt to the fitted template: every vertex near the face follows the closest point of the template, so your sculpted detail (wrinkles, pores) rides along. Vertices away from the face (the back of a bust, the shoulders) don't move.

Left and Right are the character's own: eyeBlinkLeft closes the character's left eye, which is on your right in the front view. That is what ARKit and MediaPipe mean.

Landmarks#

If the fit goes wrong (a strongly stylised face, a head turned a lot, a bust whose shoulders stick out further than the nose), guide it with landmarks:

  1. Click Auto Landmarks to see where the automatic fit puts the eye corners, the nose tip, the mouth corners and the chin.
  2. Click Place Landmarks. The canvas asks for each landmark in turn (right eye outer corner, right eye inner corner, left eye inner, left eye outer, nose tip, right mouth corner, left mouth corner, chin): click it on the sculpt. To move one, click its dot, then its new place.
  3. Click Generate Facial Shapes. With 3 or more landmarks the fit starts from them and pulls the template's landmarks onto yours; all 8 fit best.

Clear removes them (the fit is automatic again). Landmarks aren't saved with the project.

Parts#

With Parts too on, the other visible Parts get shapes by their names:

Part name containsIt gets
eye (not lash, brow or lid)the 8 eyeLook... shapes: each eye turns about its own centre (about 30 degrees sideways, 25 up or down). One Part may hold both eyes.
teeth, tooth, gum or jawthe jaw shapes (jawOpen, jawLeft, jawRight, jawForward, tongueOut): the part below the bite moves with the lower jaw, the upper teeth stay.
tonguethe jaw shapes, and tongueOut pushes it out past the lips.
anything else that sits on the face (brows, lashes, a beard, a moustache)the shapes that move the skin under it. Parts that aren't on the face get none.

The eyes' shapes are rotations stored as blend shapes, so halfway (weight 0.5) an eye is a little smaller than a true rotation would leave it, as with any blend-shape eye. Rigs that turn eyes with bones can ignore those shapes.

Mouth and eyes: what to expect#

  • An open or slit mouth (the lips separate surfaces, ideally with a mouth bag behind them) opens cleanly: jawOpen drops the lower lip and the chin, mouthFunnel and mouthPucker shape the lips.
  • A closed mouth sculpted as one continuous surface can't open: the shapes that part the lips move the lower lip and stretch the skin between the lips like a membrane. Cut the mouth first (sculpt a slit, or start from new_head, which has a mouth bag), or sculpt the fix into jawOpen.
  • Eyelids close over the eyeballs in eyeBlinkLeft / eyeBlinkRight when the eyes are a separate Part. Eyes sculpted into the head's own surface (one mesh) would be pulled along by the lids: split them into their own Part first (Split parts).
  • mouthClose is meant to be used with jawOpen: it closes the lips while the jaw is open (the ARKit convention). On its own it pushes the lips through each other.
  • tongueOut: the template has no tongue shape, so on the head it opens the jaw a little; a tongue Part moves out.

Fix a shape by sculpting#

The shapes are a starting point, like a rigger's first pass. To fix one:

  1. In Tool > Layers, select its layer (the radio button with its name).
  2. Set its weight to 1 (its slider here or in Tool > Layers) and the other shapes to 0 (Neutral below).
  3. Sculpt: every stroke goes into that shape. Use X symmetry for a centre shape; for a Left / Right pair, fix one and regenerate with Symmetry on only if you haven't fixed the other side yet.

Face test#

Under Face test:

  • Presets show common expressions built from the shapes: Neutral, Smile, Grin, Surprise, Anger, Sad, Disgust, Fear, Blink, Wink, Kiss, Aah, Ooh, Puff, Look left, Tongue out. Preset amount sets how strongly.
  • Eyes, Brows, Jaw, Mouth, Cheeks & nose, Tongue open a slider per shape. A slider drives the shape of that name on every Part (the eyes turn with eyeLookUpLeft).

Neither creates undo steps. The weights are saved with the project and written to exports (as the default pose).

Live face#

face_weights sets shape weights from a JSON object, fast enough for a live feed (30 or more calls a second):

face_weights json={"jawOpen":0.4,"eyeBlinkLeft":1,"mouthSmileRight":0.6}

It changes only the vertices those shapes move, re-uploads only those to the graphics card, makes no undo step and doesn't mark the project as changed. Names not given keep their weight; unknown names are reported. The whole face message from a phone, {"type":"face","t":...,"weights":{...},"head":[...]}, works as the JSON too. On a 700,000 vertex head whose every vertex is face, a frame that changes all 52 weights takes about 9 ms on an M1 Pro (most of it updating normals); heads with a body or shoulders change only the face.

The phone link points your phone's camera at your face and sends this feed, so the character makes your expressions (see the phone link's own chapter). Anything else that produces ARKit or MediaPipe blendshape weights can drive it too: a script (Scripting and automation), the MCP server or the Python module.

Takes#

A take is a recorded face feed: the phone link saves one as JSON frames, or write your own.

  • Load Take... plays a take on the shapes, looping (Loop), with Play / Pause. Accepted: a JSON array of face messages, an object with a "frames" array, or JSON Lines (one message per line); "t" is in milliseconds. MediaPipe's "blendshapes": [{"categoryName": ..., "score": ...}] works too.
  • Bake Take turns the loaded take into keyframes at 30 frames a second, kept in the project. Exports then carry the animation: GLB as a glTF animation of the morph target weights (Blender, three.js, Unity, Godot and Unreal play it), USD as time samples of the blend shape weights (Omniverse, Maya, Houdini, Blender). FBX exports the shapes but not the animation yet.
  • Clear Baked Take removes it.

From the console: face_take_play path=take.json, face_take_stop, face_take_bake path=take.json fps=30, face_take_clear.

Export the shapes#

The ARKit names are kept exactly in every format:

ExportThe shapes becomeAnimation (baked take)
GLB (File > Export)morph targets, named in extras.targetNamesglTF animation of the weights
USD / USDZUsdSkel blend shapestime samples on the SkelAnimation
FBX (and Send to Unreal)blend shape channels; Unreal: morph targets on a skeletal meshnot yet
Blender bridge (File > Blender > Send to Blender)shape keys, at their weights, on every objectnot yet

Each Part exports as its own object with its own shapes (the eyes with their eyeLook shapes, the teeth with the jaw's). Apple's ARKit, Unreal's Live Link Face and most face-capture tools drive meshes by these names directly.

Subdivision levels#

On a Part with subdivision levels the shapes are made on the lowest level with at least 20,000 vertices (level= picks another), and the levels carry them up: at the top level the shapes bend your high-level detail along instead of flattening it.

Memory#

Each shape is a full sculpt layer: 52 shapes add about 620 bytes per vertex (on top of what the mesh itself uses), so about 620 MB on a million-vertex head. Generate on a level of a million vertices or less and let the levels carry the shapes up.

Limits#

  • The fit expects a humanoid head with the face pointing at the front view. Animals and creatures (the lion bust) fit poorly or not at all; landmarks help with stylised humans.
  • Hair sculpted as part of the head moves with the brows and forehead; keep hair in its own Part.
  • Landmarks are placed by hand or found by the fit itself; there is no image-based face detector.
