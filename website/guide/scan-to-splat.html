Scan an object into a splat

Walk around a clay maquette, a prop or a toy with your phone, and SculptForge turns the video or photos into a Gaussian splat in the viewport. You watch it sharpen while it trains, and you can orbit around it the whole time. From there you can sculpt against it, make it sculptable, or save it for a splat viewer or a game engine. Nothing is uploaded: everything runs on your own computer.

What you need#

  • A phone or a camera. Any phone that films 1080p or better works. You don't need a special app.
  • A graphics card. Training runs on the GPU: NVIDIA, AMD or Intel on Windows and Linux, or an Apple silicon Mac. No CUDA is needed. A gaming GPU trains a Fast preview in a few minutes.
  • Three free tools, which SculptForge runs as separate programs:
ToolWhat it doesHow you get it
Brush (Apache-2.0)Trains the splatSculptForge offers to download it the first time (about 45 MB on Linux and macOS, 160 MB on Windows)
COLMAP (BSD)Works out where each photo was takenWindows: offered as a download (128 MB). Linux: your distribution's colmap package. macOS: brew install colmap
ffmpegReads frames from a videoUsually installed already; Linux: the ffmpeg package, macOS: brew install ffmpeg

Captures from the SculptForge phone link that carry the phone's own camera positions, and COLMAP or nerfstudio datasets, don't need COLMAP. Photo folders don't need ffmpeg.

Capture tips#

The capture decides the quality far more than the settings do.

  • Pick a matte object. Clay, wood, stone, painted plastic and fabric scan well. Shiny metal, glass, mirrors, water and see-through plastic don't: the trainer can't tell reflections from surface.
  • Avoid thin and fuzzy parts such as hair, fur, wire, antennas and leaves, or expect them to come out soft.
  • Use even, soft light. An overcast day, a room with daylight from several windows, or two lamps bounced off the walls. Avoid hard shadows, and don't move the lights or let your own shadow fall on the object.
  • Give it texture. A plain white object on a plain white table is hard to place; put it on a patterned cloth or a newspaper.
  • Walk all the way around. Keep the object in the middle, filling about half the frame. Go round once at eye level and once a little higher, looking down. Every part of the object should be seen from several sides.
  • Move slowly and smoothly. Each view should overlap the last by about two thirds. Blurred frames are dropped automatically, but a capture that is blurred everywhere can't be fixed.
  • Keep the object still, and don't use a turntable: the background would not turn with it.
  • Length: a 20 to 60 second video, or 40 to 150 photos.

Train a splat#

  1. Open Render > Gaussian Splats (or press F3 and type "train").
  2. Under Train from capture, pick a preset: Fast preview or Quality.
  3. Click Video... and pick the video, or Photos Folder... and pick the folder of photos. You can also drop a video file onto the window. Captures sent from the phone link start by themselves.
  4. The first time, a window asks to download Brush. It says what it downloads, from where and how big it is; nothing is downloaded until you click Download Brush. The download is checked against its SHA-256 checksum before it is used. If you already have Brush, click Choose Brush... instead.

Training then runs in the background, in three stages:

StageWhat happens
FramesA video is read at 5 frames a second and the sharpest frame of each moment is kept (80 for Fast preview, 200 for Quality). Photos are used as they are
Camera positionsCOLMAP finds where each photo was taken (skipped when the capture already knows)
TrainingBrush trains the splat. Every few hundred steps the viewport shows the latest result

The panel over the viewport shows the step, the quality so far, how long it has run and roughly how long is left:

  • PSNR compares the splat with photos it was not trained on, in decibels. Under 18 is rough, 22 to 26 is good and over 26 is sharp. A PSNR that stops rising means more training won't help much.
  • Good enough - stop ends training and keeps what it has learned so far as the result.
  • Cancel ends training and saves nothing.

You can orbit, pan and zoom while it trains. While the splat trains, the sculpt is hidden so the splat isn't inside it; tick Show mesh in Render > Gaussian Splats to bring it back.

Presets#

PresetStepsImage sizeFrames from a videoColour
Fast preview5,000up to 1024 px80simple view-dependent colour (SH 1)
Quality30,000up to 1920 px200full view-dependent colour (SH 3)

Start with Fast preview to check the capture, then train Quality from the same capture.

Where the result goes#

The finished splat is saved as a PLY next to the capture:

  • for a folder: <folder>/splat/<folder name>.ply
  • for a video: <video name>-splat/<video name>.ply beside the video

A copy goes into the Splats tab of the Library, so you can open it again later. The work folder beside the result holds the frames, the camera positions and the logs (brush.log, colmap.log); you can delete it.

The splat comes in centred and upright: SculptForge uses the camera positions to put the object at the middle of the scene with the cameras' "up" pointing up. Use Flip, Turn 90 and Fit in Render > Gaussian Splats if it still needs a turn. Then:

  • Make it sculptable.
  • Lower the opacity and sculpt against it as a reference.
  • Save splats... to .ply, .splat, .glb or .usdz for other apps.

Tools and settings#

The Tools section of Train from capture shows which tools were found. SculptForge looks for each one in this order:

  1. An environment variable: SCULPTFORGE_BRUSH, SCULPTFORGE_COLMAP or SCULPTFORGE_FFMPEG (the full path of the program). Studios can point every workstation at a shared, approved build this way.
  2. The program chosen with Choose Brush... or Choose COLMAP....
  3. A download made by SculptForge, in the tools folder of SculptForge's settings folder.
  4. brush_app, colmap and ffmpeg on the PATH.

There is no ready-made Brush for Linux on ARM (for example Asahi Linux on an Apple silicon Mac) or for Intel Macs. Build it from its source with Rust (cargo build --release in a Brush checkout) and choose the brush_app it builds.

From the command line#

splat_train works in scripts, sculptforge-cli, Python and the MCP server. There it waits until training has finished, then loads the result:

sculptforge-cli -c "splat_train source=walkaround.mp4 preset=fast; splat_save path=walkaround.glb"
ParameterMeaning
sourceA video, a folder of photos, a phone-link capture folder, or a COLMAP / nerfstudio dataset
outWhere to put the result and the work files (default: next to the capture)
presetfast or quality

In the app, splat_train starts training in the background and splat_train_cancel stops it (splat_train_cancel finish=true is Good enough - stop).

Captures with camera positions#

A capture folder can say where every photo was taken, which skips COLMAP. The phone link writes this in its capture.json; other tools can too:

{"kind": "splat", "device": "...", "files": ["IMG_0000.jpg", "..."],
 "fl_x": 1450, "w": 1920, "h": 1440,
 "frames": [{"file": "IMG_0000.jpg", "transform_matrix": [[...], [...], [...], [0, 0, 0, 1]]}]}

transform_matrix is the camera-to-world transform, row by row, with the camera looking down its -Z axis and +Y up (the OpenGL and nerfstudio convention, the same as ARKit and WebXR). fl_x, fl_y, cx, cy, w and h are in pixels and can also be given per frame. Folders that already contain a COLMAP model (images and sparse/0) or a nerfstudio transforms.json are trained as they are.

If it goes wrong#

  • "COLMAP could not place the cameras": too few views overlap, the photos are blurred, or the object is shiny or plain. Capture again more slowly, with more texture around the object. The details are in work/colmap.log.
  • "Brush stopped with an error": often the graphics card ran out of memory. Try Fast preview, close other GPU-heavy programs, or update the graphics driver. The details are in work/brush.log.
  • The splat is cloudy or full of floaters: train Quality, then use Clean floaters. Shiny and see-through parts stay cloudy whatever you do.
  • Only part of the object came out: the other side wasn't seen from enough angles. Walk around again.
