Garments: sewing patterns at real size

A garment in SculptForge is made the way clothes are made: flat pattern panels at their real size in millimetres, sewn edge to edge. The same pattern prints on your home printer to cut real fabric, and drapes on your model. Open Tool > Garments (the Pattern window shows the panels flat): Templates starts a garment at your sizes, Print writes the pages. Everything here is also a garment_* command for Scripting and the AI assistant ("make me trousers for a 1000 mm hip").

Your size chart#

Templates are drawn from a size chart saved in the project: type your measurements (or your model's) once and every template uses them. In Templates > Size chart (mm), type a value and press Enter (hover a name to see how to measure it); Default sizes puts them all back. From a script, garment_size lists the chart and takes any measurement in millimetres:

garment_size chest_mm=1020 waist_mm=860 hip_mm=1040 inseam_mm=800

Templates#

Pick one in Templates (or garment_new template=<name>): it replaces the scene's pattern with that garment at your sizes. Tick Add beside the pattern (add=true) to put it next to what is there, so one pattern can hold a whole outfit. Measurements given here go into the chart too, and length_mm sets the template's main length for this garment only.

TemplatePanelslength_mm is
tshirtfront, back, two short sleevesshoulder to hem (700)
trousersfront and back of each legthe inseam (shorts: 200 or so)
skirtstraight front and back, waistbandwaist to hem (waist to knee)
skirt_alineflared front and back, waistbandwaist to hem
hoodiefront, back, long sleeves, two-piece hood, kangaroo pocketshoulder to hem
dresssleeveless bodice, A-line skirt, joined at the waistwaist to hem
cloakhalf-circle cape in two quarters, hoodneck to hem (0.6 x height)
glovea pair: back and palm of each hand(from the hand)

Every template comes sewn the way that garment is put together, with notches where pieces meet and the seam lengths matched (garment_info lists every seam with both lengths). The hoodie's pocket is the one loose piece: topstitch it onto the front where you want it. The proportions (how much ease, how deep the armhole, how the crotch curve is shaped) are SculptForge's own, chosen to fit an average body without darts; they are a starting point to adjust, not a tailored block.

garment_size hip_mm=1050 inseam_mm=780
garment_new template=hoodie
garment_new template=trousers add=true
garment_info

Print it at home#

Print shows how many pages the pattern takes on A4 or Letter (portrait or Landscape); Save PDF... writes them all in one PDF, Save SVG pages... one SVG per page. From a script: garment_export path=trousers.pdf paper=letter landscape=true.

  • Print at 100% ("actual size", never "fit to page"), then measure the 50 mm square on page A1.
  • Each page has a name: rows A, B, C down, columns 1, 2, 3 across. Pages with nothing on them are left out.
  • Trim each page on its line at the left and the top, lay it over its neighbours' lines and match the black triangles (each page prints half of each; the neighbour's name sits next to it). Tape, then cut.
  • A panel that is another's mirror image (the other leg, the other sleeve) prints once, named for example "Left front: cut 2, 1 reversed (Right front)": fold the fabric right sides together and cut through both layers.
  • Solid lines are the sewing lines, dashed lines the cutting lines (seam allowance: 10 mm, 5 mm on gloves). Ticks across the allowance are notches; the arrow is the grain line, kept parallel to the fabric's selvedge.

For a print shop or a plotter, Export SVG writes the whole pattern as one real-size sheet; Export DXF writes the outlines for a cutting machine.
