Polygon mode: modelling with points, edges and faces

In short:

  • In the Tool panel on the right (not the Library's Tool tab), Polygon›Polygon mode turns a Part into a cage you edit polygon by polygon.
  • Pick Points, Edges or Faces, select them on the model, and move them with the Gizmo.
  • Extrude, Inset, Bevel, Edge loop and Span build on the selection, each as one undo step.

Sculpting shapes a surface; Polygon mode builds one. It is for low and medium-density meshes: a box, a cylinder, a Quad Remesh result, an imported cage. It suits hard-surface blocking, where you want flat sides, clean edges and openings in exact places, and then you sculpt the result like any other Part. You select a polygon's points, edges or faces on the model, move them with the Transform gizmo, and run an operation on them. Everything is undoable, and any brush takes you back to sculpting.

Where to find it#

Open the Tool panel on the right (not the Library's Tool tab, which holds starter meshes), then Polygon. The panel has, from the top:

  • Polygon mode, the button that turns the mode on and off.
  • Points, Edges and Faces: what a click selects.
  • All, None, Invert, Grow, Shrink, Loop, Ring and By Group: selection helpers.
  • A line that counts the selection (12 faces selected of 600), and Show hidden selection: the selected points, edges and faces on the far side of the model are drawn too, dimmer and thinner than the ones facing you, so a selection reads from the front without orbiting (on by default; unselected ones stay hidden).
  • Operations: one button per operation, each with its settings underneath.

Every button is an action you can put on a key or a shelf (Edit›Preferences›Input).

Turning it on#

Click Polygon mode (the button again, Esc or any brush goes back to sculpting). While the mode is on, the Part is drawn with its wireframe and with flat faces, so the polygons, hard edges and bevels read as built; both go back to how they were when you leave (the Part's own shading setting is untouched).

Polygon mode works on a plain mesh. When a Part can't enter it, the status line says why and what to do:

PartWhat to do first
With subdivision levelsTool›Geometry›Remove Above: the operations rebuild the cage the levels are made from
With a Micro levelRemove the Micro level (Tool›Geometry›Micro Detail)
A Forge Sphere or Tube PartMake Mesh or Bake it first: Polygon mode needs a mesh
Over 2 million trianglesQuad Remesh it (Tool›Remesh) or Decimate it (Tool›Geometry) first

Selecting#

Points, Edges and Faces pick what a click selects. A quad is one face; a quad's diagonal is no edge.

On the modelDoes
ClickSelect the point, edge or face under the cursor (only that one). The one a click would take lights up as you move.
Shift+clickAdd it.
Ctrl+clickRemove it (it lights up red).
DragSelect everything inside the box that faces you. Shift adds the box, Ctrl removes it.
Double-click an edgeSelect its edge loop: across the quads, on through every vertex with four edges, until it comes back round or meets a pole or a border.
Double-click a faceSelect its whole face group.
Click the backgroundDeselect everything.
Alt, right-dragNavigate as usual.

Switching between Points, Edges and Faces converts the selection: a face's points, a face's edges, or the faces whose points (or edges) are all selected.

  • All, None and Invert do what they say.
  • Grow adds every point, edge or face touching a selected one; Shrink takes the selection's border back (a closed edge loop keeps every edge).
  • Loop extends each selected edge to its edge loop; Ring to its edge ring, the edges across the quads beside it (what an added edge loop would cut through).
  • By Group extends the selection to the whole face groups the selected faces are in.

Selected faces are drawn in orange over the model, on the side facing you, edges as orange lines and points as orange dots. The selection belongs to the active Part. Picking another Part, or changing the mesh's polygons (remeshing, Dynamic topology, an operation that adds faces), drops it. It isn't part of undo, and it stays while you move it.

Moving the selection#

The Transform gizmo works inside Polygon mode: W Move, E Scale and R Rotate move only the selection, and the gizmo sits at its centre. Each drag is one undo step and the selection stays selected, so you can push a face out, scale it down and push again. Off the handles a drag still box-selects. Q (Draw) is back to sculpting: it puts the gizmo away and leaves Polygon mode too.

Operations#

Every operation runs on the selection and is one undo step. Delete removes the selected faces (points and edges delete the faces they fully cover). Flip turns the selected faces inside out. The modelling operations below each have their settings under their button. Drag a slider and the result previews on the model as an outline; let go and nothing has changed until you click the operation's button.

An operation that needs whole faces tells you so when only some of a face's points are selected: switch to Faces, or select whole faces. One that needs something else (edges for Edge loop, two borders for Span) says so in the status line and leaves the Part as it was.

Extrude#

Extrude pushes the selected faces out along their normals and joins them to the surface with new side walls (quads, in a face group of their own, so one double-click selects them afterwards). The cap keeps its face group, UVs and paint. With a negative Distance the faces sink in instead.

SettingDoes
DistanceHow far out (negative: in), as a fraction of the Part's size, so the same slider position means the same on a small and a big Part. 0 builds the walls flat, ready for the gizmo.
InsetNarrows the cap first, so the walls slant inward (a boss, a tapered plate), by this fraction of the selected region's narrowest width: 0.1 on one cell of a grid takes a tenth of the cell off each side, whatever the Part's size. Too deep an inset is scaled back rather than folding the cap.
IndividualEach face on its own: every face gets its own walls, with a groove down to the surface between neighbours. Off: the connected selection moves as one region, with walls only on its border.
Even thicknessWhere the selection bends (two sides of a box), every face still moves the full distance along its own normal. Off: the shared edge moves along the averaged normal and comes out less.
Keep shapeThe whole region moves along one direction (its average normal) and keeps its shape, instead of inflating along each point's normal.
Edges along normalsIn Edges: the strip stands on the surface instead of extending it.

In Edges, Extrude takes the selected open border edges (a plane's rim, the edge of a hole) and extends the surface with a strip of quads; corners go out diagonally so the strip is as wide at a corner as along an edge.

Tip: the cap stays selected, so set Distance to 0, click Extrude, then drag the cap with the gizmo (W): the shape follows your hand instead of a number, and the walls follow it.

Limit: edges with a face on both sides are left alone, so a closed mesh has no border to wall: select a whole closed mesh and the surface just moves out (an exact offset). Extrude does not work on Points.

Inset#

Inset shrinks the selected faces inward and fills the gap with a rim of quads: the way to cut a panel line, a frame or a sunken button into a flat side.

SettingDoes
ThicknessHow far the border moves in, as a share from 0 to 1 of the most the selected faces can take (0.25 by default). At 1 the inner faces keep a fifth of their size, never thinner, so a face can't turn inside out. With Each face, each polygon's own share.
DepthHow far the inner faces move along their normal, as a fraction of the Part's size: negative sinks them into the surface, positive raises them.
Each faceEvery selected face is inset by itself instead of the selection as one shape.
Rim groupThe rim joins one new face group instead of its faces' groups, so a double-click selects the rim alone afterwards.

The inner faces stay selected, so a second Inset, an Extrude or a gizmo move continues from them. The rim keeps the face group of the face it came from, UVs stay continuous (a painted texture follows), and a crease or UV seam on the old border stays on it.

Tip: select a side with a double-click, Inset with a Thickness of 0.1 to 0.25 and a negative Depth for a sunken panel, then Inset again for a second frame inside the first.

Limit: a selection that is a whole closed Part has no border to inset from; turn on Each face there.

Bevel#

Bevel rounds or chamfers the selected edges: each edge becomes a strip of faces across, and the faces on either side are cut back. With Faces picked, the border of the selection is bevelled (select one side of a box and its outline rounds, the faces inside stay as they are); if the whole Part is selected, its sharp edges (over 30 degrees) are bevelled. With Points picked, the points are cut off instead (a corner of a box becomes a triangle, or a rounded cap).

SettingDoes
WidthHow far each face is cut back, as a fraction of the Part's size.
SegmentsFaces across the strip. 1 is a flat chamfer; 3 or more reads as a rounded edge.
ProfileThe strip's shape. 0.25 is flat whatever the segments, 0.5 is round (a quarter circle on a square edge), 1 is nearly square, below 0.25 bends inward.
Limit widthHolds the width back so no two cuts meet or cross on an edge. Leave it on unless you want cuts to overlap on purpose.
New face groupThe bevel's faces get a face group of their own, so one double-click selects them again.

Where bevelled edges meet, two join in a mitre and three or more in a rounded corner (a box corner becomes a piece of a sphere). A bevel that ends in the middle of a surface keeps the vertex there; one that ends at a face cuts that face's corner round. Faces keep their face groups, UVs, mask and paint, and the bevel's faces are selected afterwards.

Tip: bevel with 3 or more Segments for a rounded edge that still catches light when you sculpt on it, or with 1 Segment for a flat chamfer; drag Width with one edge selected to see the choice before you commit.

Limit: open (border) edges and edges shared by more than two faces are left alone, and the status line counts them.

Edge loops#

Edge loop adds new loops of edges across the ring of quads through the selected edge: the quickest way to put more polygons where a model needs them (a knuckle, a neck, an edge about to be rounded). In Edges, click one edge (Ring shows the ring it would cut through) and click Edge loop: every quad of the ring is split and the new loop is selected, so a W drag moves it at once.

SettingDoes
LoopsHow many loops go into each quad of the ring, evenly spaced.
PositionWhere across the quads they go: 0.5 is centred, towards 0 or 1 they move close to either side of the ring.

Face groups, UVs, seams, creases, vertex paint and the mask carry over to the new polygons. Edges on several rings cut every one of them in one step.

Tip: to tighten a hard edge, put loops close beside it (Loops 2, Position near 0 on a ring across the edge); the new loops stay selected, so a W drag or a Scale (E) adjusts them at once.

Limit: the loop runs round the model, or stops where the ring ends: at an open border (that edge is split too), at a triangle (it is split in two, so a cylinder's cap stays closed) or at a quad the ring already crossed. A Faces or Points selection is refused with a pointer to Edges.

Span#

Span joins two edge loops or two face selections with new faces. Select the border edges of two openings (double-click an edge on each border; Shift for the second) and Span fills the gap between them with a band of quads, wound with the surface on both sides. Select faces on two sides instead and Span removes them and joins the two openings: a tunnel through a block, a handle between two shells. Select one whole border loop and Span closes it across its width (a slot gets a grid, not a fan).

SettingDoes
SegmentsRings of faces across the gap.
TwistTurns which vertex pairs with which on two loops.
ReversePairs the second border the other way round, for a piece that is wound inside out.

Borders with different numbers of edges pair by distance along each border, and the odd triangles are spread evenly round the band. The band is a face group of its own and stays selected afterwards, ready to move or smooth.

Tip: if the preview shows a crossed or pinched band, drag Twist until it runs straight, or turn on Reverse.

Limit: Span needs exactly two borders (or one closed one): with a different number the status line says so and nothing changes.

Block out, then sculpt#

  1. Start from a simple Part: Tool›Parts›Append a cube or a cylinder, or Quad Remesh a rough form so it is made of quads.
  2. Turn on Polygon mode and block out in big moves: Extrude a side, drag it with the gizmo, Inset a panel, Span two faces into a handle.
  3. Add polygons only where you need them with Edge loop, and round the hard edges with Bevel.
  4. Leave the mode (Esc) and sculpt: add subdivision levels (Add Level, Ctrl+D) or remesh, then use the brushes as usual.

Polygon mode needs a Part without subdivision levels, so model first, sculpt after. To come back to the cage later, remove the levels above it (Remove Above); the detail sculpted on them goes with them.

Undo#

Each operation, and each gizmo drag, is one undo step, and Ctrl+Z puts the Part back exactly. The selection itself is not part of undo.

Scripting#

Polygon mode is driven by three commands (the console shows every click as one of them):

CommandDoes
poly_modewhether the active Part can enter Polygon mode and its counts; element=points / edges / faces picks what is selected
poly_selectop=point / edge / face with index=N; loop / ring with an edge's index; group with group=N; rect with x0 y0 x1 y1; grow, shrink, all, none, invert, info; add / remove instead of replacing
poly_opop=list names the operations and their parameters; op=delete, op=flip, op=extrude distance=0.1, op=inset thickness=0.1 depth=-0.05, op=bevel width=0.02 segments=3, op=edge_loop count=2 position=0.3, op=span segments=2; parameters as name=value
transformselection=true moves only the selection (what a gizmo drag runs)
