Why SculptForge

I had to do something.

I'm a technologist at heart, but I work with artists every day, and I hear how much this AI moment worries them. I've seen colleagues wonder whether their craft will still have a place. SculptForge was born from that.

We're here to change how software is built: we build the orchestration layer, and our agents build the software, openly, with the people who use it. New versions ship every few days, and bug fixes sooner.

Behind the scenes, the project really does use all this new technology: agents working around the clock, weighing every idea for both its technical and its artistic merit, so it grows faster than a small team ever could. But all that power has one purpose: to remove friction, to give people starting out a good baseline, and to leave more room for what matters.

No machine will ever replace your genius, your talent and your taste.

The eye that decides where a shadow falls, the hand that feels when a form comes alive: that stays yours, and that's exactly what we want to protect.

How your feedback gets into SculptForge

Every message is read. Here is the path an idea or a bug report takes, from your words to the version on your machine.

  1. 1

    You tell us

    Post in #ideas or #bug-reports on Discord, or open a GitHub issue. Any language is fine.

  2. 2

    We answer and sort it

    You get a reply, usually within hours. Bugs go straight to a fix, free for everyone. Ideas go into the public feature queue, member requests at the top.

  3. 3

    Agents build it

    Each fix or feature gets its own build, weighed for technical and artistic merit. It runs hundreds of automated tests and is checked for speed on ordinary hardware, and every release is built and tested on Windows, macOS and Linux.

  4. 4

    A review, by eye

    Before it merges, every visible change is reviewed from screenshots: does it look right, read clearly and feel good to use? If not, it goes back.

  5. 5

    It ships, with your name

    New versions go out every few days, bug fixes sooner. We reply in your thread with the version it's in, and What's New credits you, if you'd like.
